## NATIONAL AMBIENT AIR QUALITY STANDARDS (NAAQS)

EPA establishes NAAQS for six criteria pollutants:

### Ozone (O3)
Primary standard: 70 ppb (8-hour average)
Formation: Reaction of NOx and VOCs in sunlight (secondary pollutant)
Sources: Vehicle exhaust, industrial emissions, evaporative emissions
DFW Non-attainment: Dallas-Fort Worth is a non-attainment area for ozone.
  Classified as Serious non-attainment (2023 reclassification).
  Ozone season: May 1 - October 31 in North Texas.
  TCEQ issues Ozone Action Day alerts when ozone forecast to exceed 70 ppb.
Phase I context: Industrial facilities or heavy VOC emitters near sensitive
  receptors (schools, residences) — flag for air quality assessment.

### Particulate Matter (PM2.5 and PM10)
PM2.5 standard: 9 µg/m³ (annual), 35 µg/m³ (24-hour) — tightened 2024
PM10 standard: 150 µg/m³ (24-hour)
Sources: Combustion, dust, industrial processes
Dallas County: Designated as maintenance area for PM2.5.
Construction: Major source of PM10 — fugitive dust from grading operations.
  TCEQ regulations require dust control on construction sites.

### Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2)
Standard: 100 ppb (1-hour), 53 ppb (annual)
Sources: Vehicle exhaust, combustion facilities, power plants
High traffic corridors in DFW — elevated NO2 near major highways.

### Sulfur Dioxide (SO2)
Standard: 75 ppb (1-hour)
Sources: Coal combustion, industrial processes
Less common in Texas except near industrial areas (Gulf Coast refineries).

### Carbon Monoxide (CO)
Standard: 35 ppm (1-hour), 9 ppm (8-hour)
Sources: Vehicle exhaust, combustion
Generally not a Phase I concern except near heavy traffic or industrial facilities.

### Lead (Pb)
Standard: 0.15 µg/m³ (rolling 3-month average)
Sources: Smelters, battery manufacturers, firing ranges, aviation gasoline
Phase I context: Firing ranges, battery manufacturers — flag for lead in air and soil.

## TCEQ AIR PERMITTING — FRAMEWORK

### Permit Types

NSR (New Source Review):
Major source: >100 tons/year of any criteria pollutant (in attainment areas)
  or >50 tons/year of VOCs or NOx in DFW non-attainment area.
Minor source: Below major source thresholds.

PBR (Permit by Rule):
Small, standard emission sources. Self-implementing — no TCEQ review.
Common: Small boilers, emergency generators, spray booths.

Standard Permit:
For common source categories with standardized conditions.

Flexible Permit:
For large industrial facilities with multiple emission points.
Allows facility-wide cap management.

### TCEQ Air Permit Database
All permitted air sources in Texas searchable via TCEQ.
For Phase I: Note any permitted major sources within 1 mile.
Risk classification:
- Major source with active permit + violations: HIGH
- Major source with active permit, no violations: MODERATE (proximity concern)
- Minor source or PBR: LOW (note, do not flag as REC)

## TOXIC RELEASE INVENTORY (TRI)

TRI requires facilities meeting size and chemical thresholds to report
annual releases to air, water, and land.

Key TRI parameters for Phase I:
- Total air releases (pounds/year)
- Stack vs fugitive emissions
- On-site vs off-site disposal
- Chemicals released

Phase I interpretation:
TRI presence = note in records review, NOT automatic REC.
TRI with large air releases near receptors = flag for further assessment.
TRI with carcinogens (benzene, formaldehyde, vinyl chloride) = higher concern.

Common TRI-reporting industries near DFW:
- Chemical manufacturing (Garland, Carrollton)
- Metal fabrication (Dallas, Fort Worth)
- Plastics manufacturing
- Petroleum storage and distribution
- Printing operations

## VAPOR INTRUSION — AIR QUALITY INTERFACE

Vapor intrusion (VI) creates an indoor air quality problem from subsurface contamination.
This bridges the remediation and air quality domains.

VOCs most commonly implicated:
- Tetrachloroethylene (PCE): Dry cleaners. Indoor air standard: 0.2 µg/m³ (Texas)
- Trichloroethylene (TCE): Industrial degreasers. Indoor air: 1.2 µg/m³
- Benzene: Petroleum releases. Indoor air: 0.16 µg/m³
- Vinyl chloride: Chlorinated solvent daughter product. Indoor air: 0.45 µg/m³

Texas VI screening levels:
TCEQ has developed Protective Concentration Levels (PCLs) for indoor air.
If groundwater or soil vapor concentrations exceed VI screening levels,
indoor air sampling should be conducted.

VI pathway assessment sequence:
1. Identify volatile contaminants in soil or groundwater near building
2. Apply attenuation factor (typically 0.001 for commercial, 0.03 for residential)
3. Compare calculated indoor air concentration to PCL
4. If exceeded: indoor air sampling recommended
5. If indoor air elevated: mitigation required (sub-slab depressurization)

## CONSTRUCTION AIR QUALITY COMPLIANCE

### Dust Control — TCEQ Requirements
Construction sites are significant sources of fugitive dust (PM10).
TCEQ Texas Administration Code 30 TAC 111.145:
- Control dust from unpaved roads and disturbed areas
- Water application or chemical stabilizer required
- Visible emissions from construction = violation

### DFW Non-Attainment Area Requirements
In DFW non-attainment area (Collin, Dallas, Denton, Tarrant, Ellis,
Johnson, Kaufman, Parker, Rockwall counties):
- Enhanced I/M (inspection and maintenance) for vehicles
- Stage II vapor recovery at gas stations
- Reformulated gasoline required
- Low-VOC paint and coating requirements

For large construction projects (>100 tons/year VOC or NOx):
Non-attainment NSR review required.
Most construction projects fall below this threshold.

### Asbestos — NESHAP Requirements
National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP) for asbestos.
Applies to demolition and renovation of facilities with regulated asbestos-containing
material (RACM).
Threshold: 260 linear feet of pipe insulation OR 160 square feet of friable ACM
  OR 35 cubic feet removed from off-facility components.

Phase I note: Buildings constructed before 1980 should be flagged for
potential asbestos survey prior to demolition or renovation.
This is beyond standard Phase I scope but should be noted as a recommendation.

## ODOR AND NUISANCE — TCEQ COMPLAINTS

TCEQ receives and investigates odor and air quality complaints via STEERS.
Facilities with complaint history may indicate air quality issues not captured
in permitted emission data.

Odor sources relevant to Phase I:
- Landfills: Hydrogen sulfide, methane
- Wastewater treatment: Hydrogen sulfide, ammonia
- Petroleum facilities: VOC emissions, mercaptans
- Rendering facilities: Odorous organic compounds
- Industrial facilities: Varies by process

For Phase I: Check TCEQ complaint database for any facilities within 0.5 mile.
Multiple complaints = note as air quality concern in records review.

## AIRNOW AND REAL-TIME AIR QUALITY

AirNow (airnow.gov): Real-time AQI data from EPA and state monitors.
AQI categories:
- 0-50: Good (green)
- 51-100: Moderate (yellow)
- 101-150: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (orange)
- 151-200: Unhealthy (red)
- 201-300: Very Unhealthy (purple)
- 301-500: Hazardous (maroon)

DFW ozone: Frequently reaches orange and red during ozone season (May-Oct).
Construction during red or purple days = increased regulatory scrutiny.
Field workers: Respiratory protection recommended on unhealthy air days.

TCEQ Air Monitoring Network:
Extensive network of monitors across DFW.
Data publicly available — useful for establishing local air quality baseline.

## COMMON ERRORS IN AIR QUALITY ASSESSMENT

1. Calling TRI facilities a REC: TRI reporting = note, not finding.
   TRI with nearby receptors and large releases = assessment recommendation.

2. Missing DFW non-attainment context: DFW is Serious non-attainment for ozone.
   This affects permit thresholds and construction requirements significantly.

3. Ignoring vapor intrusion in air quality context: VI is an indoor air issue
   requiring both remediation and air quality expertise.

4. Missing asbestos recommendation: Buildings pre-1980 should always receive
   asbestos recommendation note in Phase I conclusions.

5. Ignoring odor complaint history: TCEQ complaints can surface air issues
   not in formal permit databases.

6. Missing ozone season timing: Construction activities in DFW during ozone
   season have enhanced air quality implications — note in Phase I.
