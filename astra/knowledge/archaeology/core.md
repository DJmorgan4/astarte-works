## REGULATORY FRAMEWORK

### Section 106 (NHPA)
Federal undertakings must consider effects on historic properties.
Triggers: Federal permit, federal funding, federal land.
Consulting parties: Texas Historical Commission (SHPO), tribes, public.

### Texas Antiquities Code
State land protection — TPWD WMAs, GLO tracts, USACE, TxDOT ROW.
Permit required for archaeological investigation on state land.
THC (Texas Historical Commission) is lead state agency.

### ARPA (Archaeological Resources Protection Act)
Federal land — National Forests, NWRs, BLM.
Felony penalties for looting. 100-year minimum age for protection.

### NAGPRA
Human remains and sacred objects on federal land.
Inadvertent discovery: Stop work, notify agency and tribes.

## LIDAR FOR ARCHAEOLOGY IN TEXAS

### What LiDAR Detects
Burned rock middens — Hill Country circular mounds 5-50m diameter
Caddo platform mounds and burial mounds — East Texas
Paleochannels, levees, point bars — site formation context
Mission acequia (irrigation) systems — South Texas
Camino Real Spanish Colonial road alignments
Historic field patterns, structure foundations
Karst features — sinkholes, collapse features (Hill Country)

### LiDAR Data Sources (Texas)
TNRis: tnris.org — FREE Texas-specific portal, statewide coverage,
  1m to 0.5m resolution, StratMap/TxDOT/TWDB collections.
USGS 3DEP: nationalmap.gov — 1m statewide, QL2 (0.35m) expanding.
NOAA Digital Coast: coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast — Gulf Coast coverage.
OpenTopography: opentopography.org — community LiDAR + processing tools.

### Visualization Techniques
Hillshade: Multiple azimuths combined (315, 45, 135, 225 degrees)
Sky View Factor (SVF): Best for subtle topographic anomalies
Red Relief Image Map (RRIM): Excellent for Texas burned rock middens
Local Relief Model (LRM): Removes regional slope, shows micro-features
Slope map: Reveals edges of features and mounds clearly

### Software
Relief Visualization Toolbox (free): Best archaeological LiDAR tool
SAGA GIS (free): Excellent SVF and openness algorithms
QGIS (free): Basic visualization and data management
LAStools: Point cloud processing (free non-commercial)
ArcGIS Pro: Industry CRM standard

## TEXAS ARCHAEOLOGICAL SITE TYPES BY REGION

### East Texas — Caddo Cultural Area
Platform mounds, burial mounds, village sites, specialized ceremonials.
Key sites: Caddo Mounds SHS (Cherokee County), Mitchell Ridge (Galveston).
Period: AD 900-1700. THC Atlas has hundreds of recorded sites.

### Hill Country / Edwards Plateau — Central Texas Archaic
Burned rock middens: Largest concentration in North America.
  Agave/sotol roasting — distinctive circular LiDAR signature.
Rockshelters: Deep stratified limestone deposits.
Pictographs: Pecos River Style — oldest elaborate rock art in North America.
  Seminole Canyon SHP, Fate Bell Shelter, Amistad NRA.
  UNESCO World Heritage consideration.

### Paleoindian (Statewide)
Gault Site (Bell County): Most significant Clovis site in Americas.
  Buttermilk Creek Complex — pre-Clovis evidence ~15,500+ BP.
Lubbock Lake Landmark: 12,000+ year continuous occupation record.
Miami Site (Roberts County): Folsom bison kill.

### Gulf Coast
Shell middens: Karankawa coastal occupation — Aransas, Nueces, Matagorda.
Underwater sites: Sea level rise inundated Paleoindian coastal sites.
Mission period: Franciscan missions along coastal rivers (1700s).

### Trans-Pecos
Pecos River Style pictographs: Most significant rock art in Americas.
Hueco Tanks SHP: World-class Jornada Mogollon pictographs.
Big Bend petroglyphs: Rio Grande canyon sites.
Historic Apache/Comanche campsites and military sites.

## MINERAL RESOURCES — TEXAS HOT SPOTS

### Llano Uplift (Mason, Llano, Gillespie, San Saba counties)
Precambrian granite/gneiss — oldest exposed rocks in Texas.
Blue topaz (Texas state gemstone), garnet, moonstone, amethyst.
Mason County: Topaz capital of Texas.
Public collecting: Enchanted Rock State Natural Area (limited).
Fee collecting: Seaquist Ranch, Mason Mountain WMA.

### Trans-Pecos Mining District
Presidio County: Shafter Silver Mine — 30 million oz silver historically.
Brewster County: Terlingua Quicksilver District — largest mercury producer
  in US history. Cinnabar (HgS) — red mineral — open pit remnants accessible.
Culberson/Hudspeth: Salt lakes, gypsum, potash deposits.
Jeff Davis County: Agate, jasper, petrified wood — rockhound friendly.

### South Texas Uranium Belt
Karnes, Live Oak, McMullen counties.
In-situ leach uranium mining — active operations.
Groundwater monitoring required — environmental concern for Phase I ESA.

### Gulf Coast
Freeport area: Historic Frasch process sulfur production (now closed).
Salt domes: Hundreds statewide — oil/gas traps, subsidence hazard.
Silica sand: Washington, Fayette, Lee counties — active mining.

## METAL DETECTING — TEXAS LAW

Private land: Legal with landowner permission. No state restriction.
State land (TPWD, GLO, state parks): ILLEGAL without Antiquities Permit.
Federal land (NFs, NWRs, USACE): ILLEGAL — ARPA felony.
  Penalty: Up to $100,000 fine + 2 years federal prison.

### Best Legal Sites (Private Land + Permission)
Ghost towns: ghosttowns.com/states/tx — hundreds statewide documented.
Historic ranch headquarters: 19th century homesteads.
Old river crossings: Historic fords — equipment, coins.
Spanish Colonial trails: Camino Real alignments statewide.
Civil War skirmish sites: Private land portions only.

### If You Find Something Significant
Human remains: STOP immediately. Call law enforcement. NAGPRA may apply.
State land accidental find: Report to THC immediately.
Private land significant artifact: Voluntary reporting to THC encouraged.
