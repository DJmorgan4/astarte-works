- Astarte Works — design and technology advisory, ASTRA CORE AI layer
- Morgan & Mallard — heritage sporting lifestyle brand
- LithicEarth — geospatial intelligence platform

The Blue Duck Foundation is a SEPARATE 501(c)(3) — it is NOT owned by The Blue Duck LLC.
The Foundation must maintain independence from the LLC to preserve tax-exempt status.

## Texas LLC Compliance Requirements
- Annual franchise tax report due May 15 each year (Texas Comptroller)
- Registered agent must be maintained — address changes require SOS filing
- No required annual meeting but operating agreement should govern governance
- Texas LLCs do not file with SOS annually — only on changes
- Public information report (PIR) filed with franchise tax report
- Texas franchise tax: LLCs with revenue under $2.47M (2024 threshold) owe $0 but must still file

## Multi-Member vs Single-Member LLC Tax Treatment
- Single-member LLC: disregarded entity by default — income on Schedule C
- Multi-member LLC: partnership by default — Form 1065 required
- LLC can elect S-Corp status (Form 2553) for payroll tax savings above ~$40K net profit
- Holding company with subsidiary LLCs: each subsidiary is a separate disregarded entity if wholly owned

- Filed via Form 1023-EZ
- Registered in Texas — SOS File #806451017
- Focus: wetland conservation, cultural preservation, field research

## Operational Rules — What You Can and Cannot Do

### Private Benefit Prohibition
The Foundation cannot operate for the private benefit of DJ Morgan, The Blue Duck LLC, or any related entity.
- Ceto Interactive CANNOT be paid by the Foundation for services without arms-length documentation and fair market value compensation
- LithicEarth data used by the Foundation must be at fair market value or documented as in-kind donation
- DJ Morgan can be compensated as Executive Director only if Board sets compensation and documents it properly

### Lobbying Limits
- 501(c)(3) public charities may lobby but only insubstantially (generally under 5% of activities)
- No direct political campaign activity — zero tolerance, results in automatic revocation
- Conservation advocacy (wetlands protection legislation) is permissible as long as it is not direct lobbying for specific bills as the primary activity

### Unrelated Business Income Tax (UBIT)
- Income from activities unrelated to the exempt purpose is taxable
- Hunting-related revenue (if Foundation runs hunts) must be analyzed — may be related if conservation-educational, may be unrelated if primarily commercial
- Sponsorship income is generally not UBIT if no substantial return benefit beyond recognition
- Advertising income IS UBIT — distinguish from sponsorship carefully
- Passive income (interest, dividends, royalties) generally exempt from UBIT

### Annual Filing Requirements
- Form 990-N (e-Postcard): gross receipts under $50K — due 15th day of 5th month after fiscal year end
- Form 990-EZ: gross receipts $50K–$200K
- Form 990: gross receipts over $200K or assets over $500K
- Failure to file 3 consecutive years = automatic revocation of tax-exempt status
- Texas: Form 05-102 franchise tax report — nonprofits are exempt but must file for exemption

### Board Governance Requirements
- Minimum 3 board members recommended for IRS scrutiny avoidance
- Board must include independent members (not family or employees of ED)
- Conflict of interest policy required — must be documented and followed
- Board must approve Executive Director compensation annually
- Meeting minutes must be kept — at minimum annual meeting
- Financial oversight: board must review 990 before filing

### Conservation Easement Rules (Foundation holding easements)
- Foundation can hold conservation easements as a qualified organization under IRC 170(h)
- Easement must be perpetual
- Baseline documentation report required at time of donation
- Appraisal required for donor's tax deduction (not Foundation's burden, but Foundation must sign Form 8283)
- Foundation must have resources and commitment to monitor and enforce easements
- Stewardship fees from landowners are permissible and common

### Grant Compliance
- NAWCA grants: require 50% non-federal match, detailed reporting, wetland acres documentation
- ACEP/RCPP (USDA): easement-focused, Foundation must be qualified holder
- Private foundation grants TO Blue Duck Foundation: Foundation must maintain public charity status (pass public support test)
- Public support test: must receive at least 33.3% of support from public sources over 5-year period
- Large single donor giving over 2% of total support counts only at 2% for public support calculation

# PHASE I ESA CONTRACTS AND LIABILITY

## Scope of Work Language
Phase I ESA contracts must clearly define:
- Scope: ASTM E1527-21 compliant desktop + site reconnaissance + regulatory database review
- What is NOT included: Phase II, soil sampling, laboratory analysis, STEERS manual search (unless specified)
- Deliverable: Written report meeting ASTM E1527-21 format requirements
- Timeline: Typically 5-10 business days for standard residential/commercial
- Access: Client responsible for providing site access for reconnaissance

## Liability and Indemnification
Limitation of liability clause: Essential for any Phase I ESA contract.
  Recommended: Limit liability to contract fee paid.
  Example: "Consultant's total liability shall not exceed the fee paid for this assessment."
  Without this: Potential liability equals property value or cleanup costs — existential risk.

Indemnification: Client indemnifies consultant against third-party claims arising from
  client's use of report beyond stated scope.

Reliance clause: Report prepared for stated client only.
  Third-party reliance requires written reliance letter (additional fee appropriate).
  Lender reliance letter: Common request from lenders — charge $500-$1,500 per letter.

Standard of care: "Services performed consistent with standard of professional care
  exercised by practitioners of environmental consulting in Texas at the time of services."
  Do NOT use "best efforts" or "guarantee" language.

## E&O Insurance (Errors and Omissions)
E&O insurance is ESSENTIAL before taking paying Phase I ESA clients.
Without E&O: A missed LPST that causes client loss = personal liability to DJ Morgan.
Typical E&O for small environmental consulting firm:
  Coverage: $1M per claim / $2M aggregate = standard minimum
  Premium: Approximately $3,000-$6,000/year for startup firm
  Retroactive date: Must cover work from first paid project
  Tail coverage: Buy tail coverage if switching carriers — covers past work
Carriers for environmental consulting E&O:
  Philadelphia Insurance, Beazley, Chubb Environmental, AIG — compare quotes
General liability also needed: $1M/$2M — separate from E&O
  GL covers bodily injury and property damage on-site during reconnaissance
Combined GL + E&O annual premium estimate: $5,000-$10,000 for startup

## Report Deliverable Requirements (ASTM E1527-21)
Each Phase I ESA report must contain:
- Executive summary with REC findings
- Site description and current use
- Records review (regulatory database search, historical records)
- Site reconnaissance findings
- Interviews (owner, occupant, key site manager if applicable)
- EP qualifications statement
- Limiting conditions and scope limitations
- Significant data gaps with impact statements
- Conclusions and opinion (RECs/HRECs/CRECs identified)
- References
Missing any required section = non-ASTM-compliant report = potential E&O claim
