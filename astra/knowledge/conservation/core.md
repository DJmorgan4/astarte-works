## CONSERVATION EASEMENTS — FRAMEWORK

A conservation easement is a voluntary legal agreement between a landowner
and a qualified organization (land trust or government agency) that permanently
limits uses of land to protect conservation values.

Key characteristics:
- Landowner retains ownership and can sell or pass to heirs
- Restrictions run with the land — bind all future owners
- Easement holder monitors compliance in perpetuity
- Landowner receives tax benefits (deduction for donated value)

### Qualifying Organizations
Land trusts: Must be IRS-qualified under Section 501(c)(3) AND meet
  Land Trust Alliance Standards and Practices
Government agencies: Federal, state, or local

Blue Duck Foundation (EIN 41-4361489): 501(c)(3) effective February 18, 2026.
Qualified to hold conservation easements for wetland and wildlife habitat.

### Conservation Values (IRC Section 170(h))
For easement to qualify for tax deduction:
1. Scenic or open space preservation (consistent with government policy)
2. Habitat protection for endangered or threatened species
3. Outdoor recreation or education for public
4. Historic preservation
5. Agricultural land preservation

Wetland conservation: Qualifies under habitat protection AND open space.
Waterfowl habitat: Qualifies under habitat protection (migratory birds).

### Tax Benefits
Charitable deduction: Fair market value of donated easement.
Limitation: Up to 50% of AGI (100% for qualified farmers and ranchers).
Carry-forward: 15 years for excess deduction.
Estate tax: Reduces taxable estate value.

Important: IRS scrutiny of conservation easements has increased significantly.
Syndicated easements (investor-driven) are now listed transactions.
Legitimate donor-motivated easements on qualified land remain viable.

## USDA CONSERVATION PROGRAMS

### ACEP — Agricultural Conservation Easement Program

Two components:

ACEP-ALE (Agricultural Land Easements):
Protects working agricultural land from non-agricultural development.
Funds purchase of easements on farmland, rangeland, grassland.
NRCS partners with eligible entities (land trusts, state agencies).
Blue Duck Foundation could pursue ALE partnership for Texas rangeland.
Funding: Competitive — applications ranked nationally.

ACEP-WRE (Wetland Reserve Easements):
Protects and restores wetlands on agricultural land.
NRCS purchases easement and funds restoration.
30-year or permanent easement options.
VERY relevant for Blue Duck Foundation — prime program for Texas wetland conservation.
Target land: Farmed wetlands, converted wetlands, wetland buffers on agricultural land.
Texas signup: Continuous basis with periodic ranking.
Priority: Land adjacent to existing protected areas, high waterfowl value.

### NAWCA — North American Wetlands Conservation Act

Grant program for wetland habitat conservation.
Three grant types:
- Standard grants: Up to $1M federal, requires 1:1 non-federal match
- Small grants: Up to $75K, requires 1:1 match
- Canada grants: Cross-border projects

Eligible activities:
- Acquisition of wetland habitat (fee simple or easement)
- Restoration of degraded wetlands
- Enhancement of existing wetland habitat
- Management of wetland habitat

Blue Duck Foundation NAWCA priority areas:
- Playa lakes in North Texas (Central Flyway waterfowl staging)
- Trinity River floodplain bottomland hardwoods
- Coastal marsh on Gulf Coast
- Prairie pothole analogs in Blackland Prairie

Application cycle: Twice per year (March and September deadlines).
NAWCA coordinator: USFWS Division of Bird Habitat Conservation.

### CRP — Conservation Reserve Program (USDA FSA)

Pays farmers to remove environmentally sensitive land from production.
10-15 year contracts. Annual rental payments.
Practices: Grass cover, wildlife habitat, wetland restoration, riparian buffers.

CRP Continuous Signup (CCRP):
Wetland restoration practices: CP23 (wetland restoration), CP39 (farmable wetlands).
Riparian buffers: CP21, CP22.
These practices directly support waterfowl and wetland habitat.

CP23 — Wetland Restoration:
Restores drained wetlands on agricultural land.
Highly effective for playa lakes and prairie pothole restoration.
Payment: Rental rate + cost-share for restoration.

### EQIP — Environmental Quality Incentives Program

Provides financial and technical assistance for conservation practices.
Relevant practices:
- Prescribed grazing plans
- Irrigation water management
- Wetland creation and restoration
- Wildlife habitat management

## WATERFOWL HABITAT — BLUE DUCK FOUNDATION FOCUS

### Central Flyway Priority Areas
Texas is the most critical wintering state for Central Flyway waterfowl.

Playa Lakes Region (High Plains Texas):
Millions of acres of playa lakes — seasonal wetlands critical for waterfowl.
Playas threatened by drainage, cultivation, groundwater depletion.
NAWCA priority. Blue Duck Foundation opportunity area.
Target species: Pintail, mallard, teal, sandhill crane.

Texas Gulf Coast:
Rice fields, coastal marshes, reservoirs.
Major wintering area for white-fronted geese, snow geese, teal.
Less relevant for Blue Duck Foundation current focus.

North Texas Prairie (Collin, Denton, Hunt counties):
Limited remaining natural wetlands — restoration opportunity.
Trinity River corridor provides migration corridor.
High human population density — conservation near urban areas.

### Moist Soil Management
Technique: Control water levels to promote natural vegetation for waterfowl.
Draw-down: Lower water in summer to allow vegetation to grow.
Re-flood: Raise water in fall to flood vegetation for migrating waterfowl.
Results: 10-40x more waterfowl use compared to static water levels.

Plants targeted by moist soil management:
Smartweed (Polygonum): High-energy seed, preferred by dabbling ducks.
Millet (Echinochloa): High seed production, attractive to waterfowl.
Spikerush (Eleocharis): Tubers eaten by diving ducks and coots.
Chufa (Cyperus esculentus): Tubers highly preferred by wood ducks.

### Waterfowl Species — Central Flyway
Dabbling ducks (shallow water): Mallard, pintail, teal, gadwall, wigeon, shoveler.
Diving ducks (open water): Lesser scaup, ring-necked, canvasback, redhead.
Geese: Canada, white-fronted, snow, Ross's.
Special concern: Northern Pintail — below population goal since 1970s.

## CARBON MARKETS AND CONSERVATION FINANCE

Voluntary carbon markets create additional revenue for conservation landowners.

Carbon sequestration by habitat type:
Bottomland hardwood forest: 3-8 tons CO2/acre/year
Prairie restoration: 0.5-2 tons CO2/acre/year
Wetland restoration: Variable — can be source or sink depending on hydrology

Verified Carbon Standard (Verra) and Gold Standard:
Methodologies for wetland and forest carbon credits.
Verification required — adds cost but enables premium pricing.
Texas projects: Emerging — limited but growing interest.

USDA Climate-Smart Agriculture:
Recent investments in carbon market development for agriculture.
Conservation easements + carbon credits = stacked conservation finance.

## CONSERVATION EASEMENT DUE DILIGENCE — CETO ROLE

Before Blue Duck Foundation accepts a conservation easement:

Phase I ESA screening:
- Ceto regulatory database review
- CETO Risk Score — even conservation properties need baseline assessment
- Any contamination on property reduces conservation value and creates liability
- Easement holder can be liable for cleanup if they become involved in management

Baseline documentation:
- Detailed description of conservation values at time of easement
- Photographic documentation
- Vegetation mapping
- Wetland delineation
- Archaeological survey (as applicable)

Annual monitoring:
- Easement holder required to monitor compliance
- Photo documentation of key conservation areas
- Report violations to landowner

## COMMON ERRORS IN CONSERVATION ASSESSMENT

1. Missing Phase I for conservation properties: Contamination on easement land
   creates liability for Blue Duck Foundation. Always screen before accepting.

2. Ignoring stacked conservation finance: NAWCA + carbon credits + tax deduction
   can make conservation financially compelling for landowners.

3. Missing ACEP-WRE opportunity: Many Texas agricultural landowners with
   former wetlands are unaware of WRE program. Significant funding available.

4. Undervaluing playa lake restoration: Playas provide disproportionate
   ecological value relative to their size for Central Flyway waterfowl.

5. Missing TPWD Private Lands Program: Free technical assistance available
   to Texas landowners — underutilized resource for conservation planning.
