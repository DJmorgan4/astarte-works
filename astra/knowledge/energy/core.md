## TEXAS RENEWABLE ENERGY CONTEXT

Texas leads the US in renewable energy capacity:
- Wind: #1 state — over 40,000 MW installed (2024)
- Solar: #2 state and growing rapidly — over 25,000 MW installed (2024)
- ERCOT manages 90% of Texas grid — independent of national grid

Key characteristic: Texas is largely deregulated energy market.
ERCOT (Electric Reliability Council of Texas) manages grid operations.
No federal FERC jurisdiction for most Texas transmission — unique regulatory context.

### West Texas Wind
Permian Basin and Panhandle — world-class wind resource.
Wind class 6-7 in many areas (>8.0 m/s average wind speed).
Existing transmission constraints — major new lines being built (CREZ lines completed).
Land use: Large wind farms (50,000+ acres) on ranch land.
Common lease structure: $5,000-$8,000/turbine/year plus royalty.

### Texas Solar
Solar irradiance: Southwest Texas among highest in US.
West Texas, Rio Grande Plain, South Texas — best resources.
North Texas: Good resource (McKinney area: ~5.5 kWh/m²/day average).
Utility-scale solar: Growing rapidly, especially in ERCOT South zone.
Distributed solar: Residential and commercial, net metering available.

### Battery Energy Storage (BESS)
Growing rapidly in Texas post-Winter Storm Uri.
Typically co-located with solar or wind.
ERCOT ancillary services market — frequency regulation revenue.
Environmental concern: Lithium-ion battery fire risk — thermal runaway.
Fire suppression: Specialized systems required. Water alone ineffective.
Phase I context: BESS sites — assess for future battery waste and chemical release risk.

## SOLAR PROJECT ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT

### Pre-Development Environmental Screening
For utility-scale solar (>1 MW) on greenfield sites:

Step 1: Wetland and Waters screening
- NWI query for site
- FEMA flood zone — avoid Zone AE where possible
- If wetlands present: NWP 51 (Land-Based Renewable Energy) available for minor impacts
- Wetland impacts >0.5 acre: Individual permit likely

Step 2: Species and Habitat
- USFWS IPaC — generate species list for site location
- If in Central Texas: GCW habitat assessment (Ashe juniper presence)
- Migratory birds: Solar farms can affect migration — bird-friendly design practices
- Texas horned lizard: Sandy soil sites in range — assess habitat

Step 3: Cultural Resources
- NRHP (National Register of Historic Places) search
- SHPO (Texas Historical Commission) consultation if federal nexus
- Phase I archaeological survey may be required for federal permits or financing

Step 4: Agricultural Land
- USDA farmland protection — prime farmland designation
- Some lenders require mitigation for prime farmland conversion
- NLCD Class 82 (cultivated crops) — assess for prime farmland

Step 5: Stormwater
- TPDES CGP required for construction >1 acre
- SWPPP development — Ceto core competency
- Post-construction stormwater: Solar panels increase runoff, alter drainage patterns

### TCEQ Solar Permitting
Solar facilities generally have minimal air emissions.
Large inverter installations may require air permit review.
Construction phase: Standard TPDES CGP + SWPPP.
Operations: Minimal ongoing environmental compliance.

### Federal Permitting — Solar
No federal environmental permit required for private land solar without federal nexus.
Federal nexus triggers: Federal financing (DOE loans), federal land, federal permits.
NEPA required if federal nexus: Environmental Assessment or EIS.

### Phase I ESA for Solar Sites
Same standard Phase I as any commercial acquisition.
Additional considerations:
- Agricultural chemical history (if former farmland)
- Herbicide use during operations (vegetation management under panels)
- Panel washing chemicals (some contain surfactants)
- Transformer oil — PCB risk in older transformers

## WIND PROJECT ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT

### Pre-Development Environmental Screening

Avian and bat survey requirements:
- Pre-construction surveys: USFWS recommends 1-2 years of pre-construction
  avian and bat surveys for any wind project
- Eagle surveys: Bald and Golden Eagle Protection Act compliance required
  Incidental eagle take permit required if eagle mortality risk
  Eagle conservation plan required for significant eagle use areas
- Bat acoustic surveys: White-nose syndrome has affected Texas bat populations
  Cave myotis, Brazilian free-tailed bat — assess for roost near turbines

Sound and shadow flicker:
- Not federal environmental requirement but local ordinance concern
- Many Texas counties have setback requirements
- Shadow flicker: Strobe effect from rotating blades — residential concerns

### Texas Wind Lease Considerations
Wind lease due diligence overlaps with Phase I:
- Mineral rights: Are mineral rights severed? Active oil/gas production?
- Pipeline easements: High-pressure pipelines = turbine setback requirements
- Transmission access: Distance to nearest available interconnection point
- ERCOT congestion: Some West Texas zones have significant curtailment

## ERCOT GRID CONTEXT

ERCOT operates Texas grid largely independently of national grid.
Key concepts for renewable energy environmental assessment:

Interconnection queue: ERCOT has massive queue for new generation.
Congestion: Oversupply in West Texas → curtailment of wind and solar.
CREZ (Competitive Renewable Energy Zones): Transmission built to move
  West Texas renewable energy to load centers (Dallas, Houston, Austin).

Capacity factors (Texas averages):
- Wind (West Texas): 35-42%
- Solar (West Texas): 24-28%
- Solar (North Texas): 19-23%

## ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE DURING CONSTRUCTION

Ceto core competency for renewable energy construction:

SWPPP development and management:
- Large solar and wind projects disturb significant acreage
- TPDES CGP compliance throughout construction
- BMP inspection and maintenance
- Phased construction to minimize disturbed area at any time

Erosion control for solar:
- Panel installation requires grading
- East-west racking systems minimize grading vs. fixed-tilt
- Vegetation establishment under panels — seeding specs important
- Pollinator-friendly vegetation under panels gaining momentum

Stormwater design for wind:
- Access roads — unpaved roads are major erosion source
- Turbine pad drainage design
- Batch plant and laydown areas — potential contamination sources
- Concrete washout areas — require containment

## OIL AND GAS — TEXAS LEGACY CONTEXT

Texas has extensive oil and gas production history affecting environmental assessment.

Railroad Commission of Texas (RRC):
Regulates oil and gas in Texas. Not TCEQ.
Key databases:
- Well permits and completion reports
- Plugging records (abandoned wells)
- Tank battery locations
- Pipeline locations

Orphaned wells:
Texas has hundreds of thousands of orphaned (unplugged) oil and gas wells.
Orphaned wells can:
- Migrate gas (methane, H2S) to surface
- Serve as conduits for groundwater contamination
- Collapse and create surface subsidence

Phase I context:
- Any site in oil and gas producing area: Check RRC for wells on or near property
- Former tank battery location: Petroleum contamination, produced water spills
- Pipeline right-of-way: Petroleum contamination, cathodic protection issues

RRC online data: https://www.rrc.texas.gov/resource-center/research/data-downloads-historical-data/

## COMMON ERRORS IN ENERGY ASSESSMENT

1. Missing eagle survey requirement: Golden eagle in West Texas requires
   incidental take permit — missed in many Phase I reviews.

2. Ignoring bat surveys: Bat mortality at wind turbines is a significant
   regulatory issue — bat acoustic surveys increasingly required.

3. Missing RRC well check for oil and gas areas: Orphaned wells on solar
   or wind sites can be major liability — RRC check required.

4. Underestimating SWPPP complexity for large projects: 1,000-acre solar
   farm requires sophisticated phased SWPPP — Ceto opportunity.

5. Missing prime farmland assessment: Some financiers require prime farmland
   mitigation for solar development — assess early.

6. Ignoring BESS thermal runaway risk: Battery storage fires create
   significant environmental release risk — flag in any Phase I.
