## TEXAS STRATIGRAPHY — FRAMEWORK

Texas geology is exceptionally diverse — from Precambrian basement in West Texas
to Quaternary alluvium along the Gulf Coast.

### GEOLOGICAL PROVINCES OF TEXAS

Gulf Coastal Plain (East and South Texas):
Thick Cretaceous through Cenozoic sedimentary sequence dipping SE toward Gulf.
Includes Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford, Woodbine in north; younger Tertiary sands south.
Subsidence from groundwater withdrawal affects flood zone elevations (Houston area).

Edwards Plateau (Central Texas — Hill Country):
Flat-lying Cretaceous limestone and dolomite. Edwards and associated limestones.
Highly karstified. Edwards Aquifer recharge zone. Most sensitive geological
province in Texas for groundwater contamination. Any LPST here = escalate.

Llano Uplift (Central Texas — Llano, Mason, Gillespie counties):
Exposed Precambrian basement — granite, gneiss, schist. Low primary permeability.
Surrounded by Paleozoic and Cretaceous rocks. Springs common at contact zones.

Permian Basin (West Texas):
Thick Permian evaporites, carbonates, and clastics. Oil and gas province.
Dissolution of evaporites (gypsum, halite) causes active subsidence and sinkholes.
Wink Sinks (Winkler County): dramatic ongoing collapse. Unique hazard.

North Texas / DFW Metroplex:
Cretaceous sequence — Woodbine sand, Eagle Ford shale, Austin Chalk,
Taylor marl, Navarro Group. Blackland Prairie soils on Taylor/Austin.
Most Phase I ESA activity in Texas occurs in this province.

Trans-Pecos (Far West Texas):
Igneous and metamorphic rocks, Basin and Range structure, volcanic fields.
Limited groundwater, high mineral content where present.

## NORTH TEXAS FORMATIONS — DETAILED

The DFW metroplex sits on a Cretaceous sequence. From oldest to youngest:

### Woodbine Formation (Cenomanian — ~95 Ma)
Sand, sandstone, and minor shale. A major aquifer in North Texas.
Depth in Dallas area: 300-600 feet below surface.
Permeability: HIGH — sand aquifer, productive for water supply.
Surface outcrop: Tarrant, Denton, Dallas counties along Woodbine escarpment.
Environmental concern: LPST releases where Woodbine is shallow can reach this aquifer.
Phase II scope: If Woodbine within 100 feet of surface near LPST — deeper investigation warranted.
Water supply significance: Many municipal supply wells in Woodbine. Impact = major regulatory concern.

### Eagle Ford Shale (Cenomanian-Turonian — ~93 Ma)
Dark, organic-rich calcareous shale. Oil/gas production in South Texas.
North Texas characteristics:
- Relatively impermeable — acts as aquitard between Woodbine and Austin Chalk
- Weathers to dark, sticky clay similar to Houston Black
- Low hydraulic conductivity = slow matrix migration
- Fractures and joints provide preferential flow paths
- Typically 50-150 feet thick in DFW area
Environmental significance: Aquitard protects Austin Chalk from overlying Woodbine contamination.
BUT: If Eagle Ford is fractured or faulted, aquitard function compromised.

### Austin Chalk (Coniacian-Campanian — ~85-90 Ma)
White to light gray chalky limestone. Dominant bedrock across DFW and Blackland Prairie.
Characteristics:
- Chalk texture — soft, porous, friable limestone (can be hand-dug in many areas)
- Moderate to high primary porosity but LOW primary permeability
- FRACTURE PERMEABILITY dominant — water and contaminants move through fracture networks
- Karst features possible where fractures enlarged by dissolution
- Locally important aquifer in fractured zones
Depth in DFW area: 20-100 feet below land surface
Depth in McKinney/Collin County: approximately 30-80 feet typical
Depth in Dallas: 50-150 feet typical

CRITICAL PHASE I IMPLICATION:
LPST on thin soils over Austin Chalk → petroleum reaches fracture network.
BTEX in fractured chalk migrates rapidly and UNPREDICTABLY.
Standard porous media plume models DO NOT APPLY to fractured Austin Chalk.
Phase II scope must include: oriented core, packer tests, fracture analysis.
Tracer tests may be required to characterize fracture connectivity.

### Taylor Marl (Campanian — ~75-80 Ma)
Dark gray to olive calcareous marl and chalk. Overlies Austin Chalk.
Primary parent material for Houston Black and related Vertisol soils.
Characteristics:
- Soft, plastic when wet — very low permeability
- Aquitard above Austin Chalk — natural protection where thick (>30 ft)
- Where thin or eroded: less protection of underlying chalk
- Weathers to heavy expanding clay soils
Thickness: Highly variable — 0 to 200+ feet across DFW area

### Navarro Group (Maastrichtian — ~66-72 Ma)
Upper Cretaceous chalks, marls, and limestones. Caps Cretaceous sequence.
Less commonly encountered in surface Phase I investigations.
Present in far north Collin County and northeast Dallas area.

### Quaternary Alluvium
River and creek deposits — sand, gravel, silt, clay. Present along all major streams.
Thickness: 5-50+ feet along Trinity River system; thinner along smaller creeks.
Permeability: HIGH where sand and gravel dominated.
Water table: Typically shallow (5-20 feet) in alluvial settings.

CRITICAL FOR PHASE I:
Alluvial aquifer along creek corridors = FIRST aquifer impacted by surface contamination.
Shallow water table + high permeability + direct connection to surface water.
Any LPST in alluvial setting = higher migration concern than upland clay setting.
Flooding can mobilize contamination laterally and redistribute plume.

## KARST GEOLOGY — ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

Karst = terrain formed by dissolution of soluble rocks (limestone, dolomite, gypsum).
Three major karst regions in Texas — each with distinct Phase I implications.

### Edwards Plateau Karst (Central Texas)
Most significant karst system in Texas.
Formations: Edwards Limestone, Georgetown, Comanche Peak, Walnut.
Features: Springs, sinkholes, caves, disappearing streams, losing streams.
Edwards Aquifer: One of most productive and most vulnerable aquifers in US.
Recharge zone: Where Edwards crops out — Kinney County NE to Bell County.
Contributing zone: Slightly less sensitive area upgradient of recharge zone.

MANDATORY ESCALATION IN EDWARDS RECHARGE ZONE:
Any LPST, RCRA facility, or spill in Edwards recharge zone = IMMEDIATE high priority.
Essentially NO attenuation between surface and aquifer in recharge zone.
Contamination reaches aquifer rapidly through karst conduits.
TCEQ and TWDB treat Edwards contamination with highest regulatory priority.
Phase II discussion is essentially mandatory for any REC in recharge zone.
Coordinate with TCEQ Region 13 (San Antonio) for recharge zone sites.

CONTRIBUTING ZONE: Additional protection area — less stringent than recharge but still elevated.
Development in EARZ requires TCEQ authorization and water quality protection plan.

### Austin Chalk Karst (North and Central Texas)
Less developed than Edwards but present and significant.
Dissolution along fractures creates voids, cavities, solution-enlarged joints.
Sinkhole risk: MODERATE in areas of thick Austin Chalk with thin soil cover.
Features to note:
- Springs emanating from chalk
- Sinkholes (circular depressions in aerial photography or DEM data)
- Losing stream reaches (stream loses flow to subsurface)
- Dry creek beds that carry flow only after heavy rain

DFW KARST INDICATORS:
Thin Altoga soils over Austin Chalk → karst possible
Tarrant soils (rock at surface) → karst likely present
Circular topographic depressions on DEM hillshade → investigate
Springs in creek channels flowing from chalk exposure → karst indicator

### Permian Basin Evaporite Karst (West Texas)
Dissolution of Permian gypsum and halite.
Wink Sinks: Active ongoing collapse in Winkler County — dramatic surface failure.
Mechanism: Evaporite dissolution at depth → void formation → sudden collapse.
Risk: Relevant for ANY site in Permian Basin / West Texas.
Phase I note: Evaporite subsidence hazard should be noted for West Texas sites.

### Karst Risk Assessment Protocol

REMOTE INDICATORS — check for all limestone/dolomite sites:
Topographic: Closed contour depressions in DEM, irregular drainage, dry valleys.
Aerial: Circular features, disturbed vegetation patterns, livestock congregation areas.
Geologic: Macrostrat returns limestone/dolomite/chalk, site in known karst province.
Soil series: Altoga or Tarrant soils indicate thin soil over carbonate rock.

RISK CLASSIFICATION:
MAPPED KARST: Site on Edwards recharge zone or known karst feature → VERY HIGH
POSSIBLE KARST: Limestone formation, DFW setting, no mapped features → MODERATE
LIMESTONE PRESENT: Formation present but buried or non-karstified context → LOW-MODERATE
KARST UNLIKELY: Shale, clay, deep limestone, clastic rocks → LOW

## MACROSTRAT DATA INTERPRETATION

Macrostrat provides regional formation data at ~1:500,000 scale.
Site-specific geology ALWAYS requires borings — Macrostrat is screening only.

Key fields:
name: Formation name
lith: Lithology (chalk, shale, sandstone, limestone, alluvium, etc.)
b_age / t_age: Age in Ma
age: Geologic period name
descrip: Text description
comments: Additional context

### LITHOLOGY INTERPRETATION TABLE

LIMESTONE / CHALK / DOLOMITE:
→ Assess karst potential (especially in Central Texas)
→ Note fracture permeability — standard porous media migration assumptions invalid
→ Flag if thin soil cover (Altoga, Tarrant series)
→ Austin Chalk: fracture network contamination pathway
→ Edwards Limestone: escalate — mandatory aquifer protection

SHALE / MARL / CLAY (Eagle Ford, Taylor):
→ Aquitard function — generally slow matrix migration
→ Fractures create preferential pathways despite low matrix permeability
→ Limited karst concern (non-soluble)
→ Engineering: high shrink-swell where weathers to clay

SAND / SANDSTONE (Woodbine):
→ HIGH permeability — rapid contaminant migration
→ Major aquifer concern where shallow
→ Limited natural attenuation capacity
→ Phase II must reach formation if LPST within influence area

IGNEOUS / METAMORPHIC (Precambrian, Trans-Pecos):
→ Low primary porosity and permeability
→ Fracture permeability controls flow
→ Limited aquifer potential but springs at contacts

ALLUVIUM (Quaternary):
→ Highest variability — depends on sand/gravel vs clay content
→ Often shallowest, most vulnerable aquifer
→ Direct connection to surface water in floodplain
→ Water table typically shallow (5-20 ft)
→ Phase II scope: monitoring wells in alluvium if LPST in floodplain

EVAPORITES (Permian — West Texas):
→ Active dissolution hazard
→ Subsidence risk
→ Permeable conduits where dissolved

UNKNOWN / NO DATA:
→ Document as data gap
→ Recommend geotechnical boring or public water well log review (TWDB)
→ USGS geological quadrangle maps as backup source

## TEXAS WATER WELL DATABASE — TWDB

Texas Water Development Board maintains database of water well logs.
URL: https://www.twdb.texas.gov/groundwater/data/gwdbrpt.asp
Useful for: Formation depths, water table depths, nearby well yields.
Phase I use: Identify shallow wells within 0.5 mile — potential receptors.
Well logs show: Driller's log of formations encountered — proxy for site geology.
Limitation: Variable quality — old logs may have poor formation descriptions.

## FAULT AND LINEAMENT CONTEXT

Texas faults relevant to Phase I:
Balcones Fault Zone: NE-SW trending fault system from Del Rio to Austin to Waco.
  Controls transition between Edwards Plateau and Gulf Coastal Plain.
  Offset creates springs and controls groundwater flow.
  Sites near Balcones faults: Assess fracture permeability carefully.

Mexia-Talco Fault Zone: East Texas. Growth faults in Cretaceous section.
  Less relevant to most Phase I investigations.

Normal faults (DFW area): Minor normal faults associated with Austin Chalk.
  Can offset aquifer units, create windows through aquitards.
  Not commonly mapped at site scale but may affect Phase II scope.

## SUBSURFACE INVESTIGATION METHODS — PHASE II CONTEXT

Geology-driven Phase II scope decisions:

Shallow soils and alluvium: Standard soil borings, monitoring wells, MIP logging.
Woodbine sandstone (sand aquifer): Monitoring wells screened in formation, full BTEX+MTBE.
Austin Chalk (fractured): Oriented core, packer tests, fracture trace analysis,
  tracer tests, consider geophysics (electrical resistivity, GPR).
  Standard soil/groundwater approach = INSUFFICIENT for fractured chalk.
Edwards Limestone (karst): Karst hydrogeology specialist, dye tracing,
  spring inventory, geophysics (ERT, seismic), void detection.
Permian Evaporites: Subsidence hazard assessment, void mapping, GPR.
Quaternary alluvium: Shallow wells, direct push sampling, water table monitoring.

## COMMON ERRORS IN GEOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

1. Applying porous media migration to fractured Austin Chalk — standard plume
   models produce completely wrong results. Fracture network = channeled, deep,
   irregular plumes. Must note in Phase I recommendations.

2. Missing karst indicators in DEM data — circular depressions visible in 1m
   hillshade are often missed in desk reviews. Check hillshade for all limestone sites.

3. Treating Macrostrat as site-specific — Macrostrat is regional (1:500,000 scale).
   Do not use it to characterize site-specific depth to formation.

4. Ignoring formation age and regional context — Cretaceous limestone in Hill Country
   (Edwards) carries very different risk than same formation buried under 200 ft of clay.

5. Missing Edwards recharge zone — most critical geological escalation in Texas.
   Any LPST in recharge zone requires immediate escalation in Phase I findings.

6. Confusing Austin Chalk softness with impermeability — chalk IS porous but primary
   permeability is low. Fractures dominate flow. Soft texture does not mean slow migration.

7. Ignoring alluvial overburden — Macrostrat shows bedrock formation but 10-30 ft of
   alluvium may overlie it. Borings always needed for site-specific depth determination.

8. Missing Woodbine aquifer depth in DFW assessments — if Woodbine is within 200 ft
   of surface near a known LPST, deeper Phase II scope must be considered.

9. Not flagging Balcones Fault Zone proximity — fault zone creates fractured rock
   and spring systems that change Phase II investigation approach.

10. Assuming TWDB well logs accurately describe formation depths — driller logs vary
    in quality. Use as guidance, not definitive stratigraphy.

## EDWARDS RECHARGE ZONE — MANDATORY ESCALATION PROTOCOL

If site is within Edwards Aquifer recharge zone:
1. Flag immediately — regardless of CETO Score or other findings
2. Any LPST, dry cleaner, or industrial facility = Potential REC at minimum
3. Recommend Phase II ESA discussion for any transaction involving contamination
4. Note TCEQ and SAWS (San Antonio Water System) oversight jurisdiction
5. Note TCEQ Region 13 (San Antonio) as relevant regional office
6. Distinguish contributing zone (less stringent) from recharge zone (most stringent)
7. Note TCEQ EARZ permit requirements for development activities
