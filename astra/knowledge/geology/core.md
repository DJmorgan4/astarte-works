## TEXAS STRATIGRAPHY — FRAMEWORK

Texas geology is exceptionally diverse — from Precambrian basement in West Texas
to Quaternary alluvium along the Gulf Coast. Understanding the regional context
of any site requires knowing which geological province it occupies.

### GEOLOGICAL PROVINCES OF TEXAS

Gulf Coastal Plain (East and South Texas):
Thick sequences of Cretaceous through Cenozoic sedimentary rocks dipping
southeast toward the Gulf. Includes the Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford, and Woodbine
in the north and younger Tertiary sands and clays to the south.

Edwards Plateau (Central Texas — Hill Country):
Flat-lying Cretaceous limestone and dolomite. Edwards and associated limestones.
Highly karstified. Edwards Aquifer recharge zone. Most sensitive geological
province in Texas for groundwater contamination.

Llano Uplift (Central Texas — Llano, Mason, Gillespie counties):
Exposed Precambrian basement — granite, gneiss, schist. Surrounded by
Paleozoic and Cretaceous rocks. Unique geological window.

Permian Basin (West Texas):
Thick Permian evaporites, carbonates, and clastics. Oil and gas province.
Dissolution of evaporites causes ongoing subsidence and sinkhole formation.
Unique geological hazard not found elsewhere in Texas.

North Texas / East Texas (Dallas-Fort Worth area):
Cretaceous sequence — Woodbine sand, Eagle Ford shale, Austin Chalk,
Taylor marl, Navarro Group. Blackland Prairie soils developed on Taylor/Austin.

Trans-Pecos (Far West Texas):
Igneous and metamorphic rocks, Basin and Range structure, volcanic fields.

## NORTH TEXAS FORMATIONS — DETAILED

The DFW metroplex sits on a Cretaceous sequence. From oldest to youngest:

### Woodbine Formation (Cenomanian — ~95 Ma)
Sand, sandstone, and minor shale. A major aquifer in North Texas.
Depth in Dallas area: 300-600 feet below surface.
Permeability: HIGH — sand aquifer, productive for water supply.
Contamination concern: LPST releases in areas where Woodbine is shallow
can reach this aquifer. Major aquifer protection concern.
Surface expression: Outcrops in Tarrant, Denton, Dallas counties along
the Woodbine escarpment (west of the Balcones fault zone equivalent).

### Eagle Ford Shale (Cenomanian-Turonian — ~93 Ma)
Dark, organic-rich calcareous shale. A major oil and gas production zone
in South Texas but also present in North Texas as a non-producing unit.
Characteristics in North Texas:
- Relatively impermeable — acts as aquitard
- Weathers to dark, sticky clay — similar behavior to Houston Black
- Low hydraulic conductivity = slow contaminant migration through matrix
- BUT fractures and joints provide preferential flow paths
Surface expression: Outcrops in a band from North Texas southwest.
Environmental significance: Aquitard between Woodbine sand and Austin Chalk.

### Austin Chalk (Coniacian-Campanian — ~85-90 Ma)
White to light gray chalky limestone. THE dominant bedrock formation
across the DFW metroplex and Blackland Prairie.
Characteristics:
- Chalk texture — soft, porous, friable limestone
- Moderate to high primary porosity but LOW primary permeability
- FRACTURE PERMEABILITY dominant — water and contaminants move
  through fracture networks, not matrix pores
- Produces water in fractured zones — a locally important aquifer
- Karst features possible where fractures are enlarged by dissolution
Depth in DFW area: 20-100 feet below land surface in many areas
Depth in McKinney/Collin County: approximately 30-80 feet typical

CRITICAL PHASE I IMPLICATION FOR AUSTIN CHALK:
When an LPST is on thin soils over Austin Chalk, petroleum hydrocarbons
that reach bedrock enter the fracture network. BTEX in fractured chalk
can migrate rapidly — plumes in fractured rock are NOT predictable using
standard porous media assumptions. Phase II scope must account for
fractured rock investigation (oriented core, packer tests, tracer tests).

### Taylor Marl (Campanian — ~75-80 Ma)
Dark gray to olive calcareous marl and chalk. Overlies Austin Chalk.
The primary parent material for Houston Black and related soils.
Characteristics:
- Soft, plastic when wet — very low permeability
- Acts as aquitard above Austin Chalk
- Where thick, provides natural protection of Austin Chalk aquifer
- Weathers to heavy Vertisol clay soils

### Navarro Group (Maastrichtian — ~66-72 Ma)
Upper Cretaceous chalks, marls, and limestones. Caps the Cretaceous sequence.
Less commonly encountered in DFW surface investigations.

### Quaternary Alluvium
River and creek deposits — sand, gravel, clay. Present along all major streams.
Highly variable thickness: 5-50+ feet along Trinity system.
HIGH permeability where sand and gravel dominated.
Critical for Phase I: Alluvial aquifer along creek corridors is often
the first aquifer impacted by surface contamination. Shallow water table,
high permeability, direct connection to surface water.

## KARST GEOLOGY — ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

Karst is terrain formed by dissolution of soluble rocks — primarily limestone,
dolomite, and gypsum. Texas has three major karst regions.

### Edwards Plateau Karst (Central Texas)
The most significant karst system in Texas.
Formations: Edwards Limestone, Georgetown, Comanche Peak, Walnut.
Features: Springs, sinkholes, caves, disappearing streams, losing streams.
Edwards Aquifer: One of the most productive and most vulnerable aquifers in the US.
Recharge zone: Where Edwards crops out at surface — extends from Kinney County
northeast to Bell County.

PHASE I IMPLICATIONS IN EDWARDS RECHARGE ZONE:
Any LPST, RCRA facility, or spill in the Edwards recharge zone is an IMMEDIATE
high-priority finding. There is essentially NO attenuation between surface and
aquifer in the recharge zone. Contamination reaches the aquifer rapidly.
Regulatory response: TCEQ and TWDB treat Edwards recharge zone contamination
with highest priority. Phase II is essentially mandatory.

### Austin Chalk Karst (North and Central Texas)
Less developed than Edwards but present.
Dissolution along fractures creates voids, cavities, and solution-enlarged joints.
Sinkhole risk: MODERATE in areas of thick Austin Chalk with thin soil cover.
Features to note: Springs emanating from chalk, sinkholes (look for
circular depressions in aerial photography), losing stream reaches.

### Permian Basin Evaporite Karst (West Texas)
Dissolution of Permian gypsum and halite creates:
- Wink Sinks (Winkler County) — dramatic surface collapse
- Ongoing subsidence over Permian evaporites
- NOT relevant to DFW/Central Texas environmental assessment
but significant for any West Texas site evaluation.

### Karst Indicators — Remote Assessment

From topographic data:
- Circular depressions: Potential sinkholes or doline features
- Closed contours (depressions in otherwise flat terrain)
- Spring locations mapped by TPWD or TWDB
- Losing stream reaches (stream disappears into ground)

From geology:
- Macrostrat returns limestone, dolomite, or chalk formation
- Site in or near Edwards Plateau
- Altoga soils over Austin Chalk (Altoga indicates thin soil over chalk)
- Tarrant soils (extremely thin soil over limestone)

Risk assessment:
MAPPED KARST: Site on known karst formation with documented features → HIGH risk
POSSIBLE KARST: Limestone formation present, no mapped features → MODERATE risk
KARST UNLIKELY: Shale, clay, or deep limestone → LOW risk

## MACROSTRAT DATA INTERPRETATION

Macrostrat is a global geological database providing formation data by location.
Key fields returned for any coordinate:

name: Formation name (Austin Chalk, Eagle Ford Shale, etc.)
lith: Lithology description (chalk, shale, sandstone, limestone, etc.)
b_age / t_age: Bottom and top age in Ma (millions of years ago)
age: Age name (Cretaceous, Paleozoic, etc.)
descrip: Text description of formation characteristics
comments: Additional geological context

Interpreting Macrostrat for environmental assessment:

LIMESTONE / CHALK / DOLOMITE:
→ Assess karst potential
→ Note fracture permeability — standard porous media migration doesn't apply
→ Flag if thin soil cover (Altoga, Tarrant series) — rapid contaminant migration

SHALE / MARL / CLAY:
→ Low primary permeability — aquitard function
→ Fractures can create preferential pathways
→ Less karst concern but not zero

SAND / SANDSTONE / GRAVEL:
→ High permeability — rapid contaminant migration
→ Good aquifer but vulnerable
→ Woodbine sandstone in North Texas = major aquifer concern

IGNEOUS / METAMORPHIC (Precambrian):
→ Low primary porosity and permeability
→ Fracture permeability controls
→ Limited aquifer potential

ALLUVIUM (Quaternary):
→ Highest variability — depends on sand/gravel vs clay content
→ Often shallowest and most vulnerable aquifer
→ Direct connection to surface water in floodplain position

Unknown / No data:
→ Document as data gap
→ Recommend geotechnical boring or public well log review

## SUBSURFACE INVESTIGATION METHODS — PHASE II CONTEXT

When geology drives Phase II scope:

Shallow soils and bedrock: Geotechnical borings, test pits, soil gas survey
Sand aquifer (Woodbine): Monitoring wells, groundwater sampling, MIP logging
Fractured limestone/chalk (Austin Chalk): Oriented core, packer tests,
  tracer tests, fracture analysis — standard soil/groundwater approach insufficient
Karst (Edwards): Specialized karst hydrogeology investigation — dye tracing,
  spring inventory, geophysical survey (ERT, GPR), void mapping

## COMMON ERRORS IN GEOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION

1. Applying porous media migration to fractured rock:
   Standard BTEX plume models don't work in fractured Austin Chalk.
   Plumes in fractured rock are irregular, channeled, and often deeper.

2. Missing karst indicators: Circular topographic depressions visible in DEM
   data are often overlooked in desk reviews. Always check hillshade for closed
   contour depressions.

3. Ignoring formation age context: A Cretaceous limestone in Central Texas
   carries very different risk than a Cretaceous limestone in East Texas (buried).

4. Treating Macrostrat as site-specific: Macrostrat provides regional formation
   data at 1:500,000 scale or coarser. Site-specific geology requires borings.

5. Missing the Edwards recharge zone: Sites within the Edwards recharge zone
   require immediate escalation regardless of LPST status or CETO Score.

6. Confusing Austin Chalk with hard limestone: Austin Chalk is SOFT and friable —
   it behaves differently from hard limestones. It can be hand-dug in many areas.

7. Ignoring alluvial overburden: Many sites have 10-30 feet of alluvium over bedrock
   that Macrostrat does not capture. Borings always needed for site-specific depth.

## EDWARDS RECHARGE ZONE — MANDATORY ESCALATION PROTOCOL

If a site is within the Edwards Aquifer recharge zone:

1. Flag immediately regardless of other CETO Score components
2. Note: TCEQ and SAWS (San Antonio Water System) maintain strict oversight
3. Any LPST, dry cleaner, or industrial facility within recharge zone = POTENTIAL REC
   at minimum regardless of regulatory status
4. Recommend Phase II ESA discussion for any transaction
5. Coordinate with TCEQ San Antonio regional office (Region 13)
6. Note contributing zone (additional protection) vs recharge zone
