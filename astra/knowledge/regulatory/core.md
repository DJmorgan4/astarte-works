## CORE FRAMEWORK — ASTM E1527-21

The 2021 revision is the controlling standard. Key changes from E1527-13:
- Vapor intrusion explicitly a recognized condition
- PFAS must be noted as beyond standard scope
- Significant data gap definition tightened — must state impact on REC identification
- Historical research sources expanded — aerials, Sanborn maps, city directories required
- EP qualifications explicitly defined — must meet one of four criteria
- Practical difficulty vs. significant data gap distinction clarified

EP QUALIFICATION CRITERIA (ASTM E1527-21 Section 3.2.28):
An EP must meet ONE of:
1. Licensed PE or PG with environmental experience
2. Degree in related science + 3 years environmental experience
3. 10 years environmental experience
4. Combination of education and experience

## THE FOUR CONDITIONS

REC — Recognized Environmental Condition
Presence or likely presence of hazardous substances due to release or likely release.
Does NOT require confirmed contamination — requires reasonable basis to believe release occurred.
Must identify: source, substance, pathway, and receptor potential.

HREC — Historical REC
Past REC remediated to regulatory closure. Requires documented closure letter.
Without closure letter = still a REC, not an HREC.
Interim closure letter = still active, still a REC.
Conditional closure with AULs = CREC, not HREC.

CREC — Controlled REC
Remediated but with residual contamination under institutional or engineering controls.
Common for LPST sites with deed restrictions or AULs.
CREC requires: documentation of controls, statement that controls are in place and effective.
Reviewer must verify AUL/deed restriction recorded in county records.

De Minimis Condition
Does not present threat to human health. Would not trigger enforcement action.
Small, isolated, well-characterized releases with no migration evidence.
De Minimis requires: small quantity, low mobility substance, isolated, documented.
Do not use De Minimis for uncharacterized or unmeasured releases.

PHREC — Potential HREC (new in 2021)
A condition that may meet HREC criteria but documentation is incomplete.
Use when closure appears likely but records cannot be confirmed.

## REC CLASSIFICATION RULES

CONFIRMED REC requires:
- Documented release in regulatory record (LUST, LPST, spill report, enforcement)
- OR field evidence of release (staining, odors, UST presence, drums)
- OR known on-site contamination from regulatory database

POTENTIAL REC requires ALL of:
- Regulatory listing WITH compliance action (not just permit presence)
- Proximity within ASTM search radii
- Plausible migration pathway to subject property
- No documented closure

PROXIMITY CONCERN when:
- Facility in regulatory database
- Within search radius
- No compliance action, no release documentation, no migration evidence
- DO NOT call this a REC

NEVER:
- Escalate Proximity Concern to Potential REC based on proximity alone
- Say no contamination found — say no evidence of release within scope of services
- Call a site clean — say no RECs identified within scope of this assessment
- Call something a REC because it looks bad without regulatory evidence
- Use "may be contaminated" without regulatory basis
- State Phase I findings as confirmed without Phase II data

## EPA ECHO INTERPRETATION

NPDES — National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System
Presence alone = LOW risk (permitted discharge, not contamination)
Active violation = MODERATE, flag for review
Significant Non-Compliance (SNC) = HIGH, document and assess migration pathway
Formal enforcement action = document and assess as Potential REC

RCRA — Resource Conservation and Recovery Act
LQG (Large Quantity Generator) within 1 mile = assess seriously, check compliance
SQG within adjoining = note, assess compliance status
CORRACTS (cleanup required by EPA) = treat as Potential REC regardless of distance within 1 mile
Generator status alone no violations = Proximity Concern only
Hazardous waste manifest violations = flag, document

TRI — Toxic Release Inventory
Self-reported annual releases to air, water, land.
Presence = note in records review, not automatic REC.
Large chronic releases (>1000 lbs/yr) near subject property = flag for assessment.
TRI substances include: benzene, toluene, xylene, TCE, PCE, chromium, lead, arsenic.

ERNS — Emergency Response Notification System
On-property or adjacent = assess as Potential REC
Substance + quantity + distance drive the tier
Spill report with confirmed cleanup = document status
Spill report no cleanup documentation = Potential REC

SEMS / CERCLIS — Superfund sites
SEMS Active = treat as Potential REC if within 0.5 mile
SEMS Archive = document, assess current status
NPL = treat as REC if within 1 mile
NPL with ROD (Record of Decision) complete = assess completion status

## TCEQ DATABASE INTERPRETATION

PST Program — Petroleum Storage Tanks:
Active registered tank no release = Proximity Concern
LPST file opened no closure = Potential REC
LPST within 0.5 mile no closure letter = Potential REC, document migration pathway
LPST within 0.25 mile no closure = strong Potential REC
LPST with INTERIM closure = still active REC — interim is not final
LPST with FINAL closure letter = HREC (verify final not interim)
LPST with Certificate of Completion = HREC
LPST with AUL/deed restriction = CREC

PST STATUS CODES — TCEQ field values:
CA = Corrective Action — active cleanup, treat as REC
IC = Interim Closure — not closed, treat as Potential REC
FC = Final Closure — closed, treat as HREC if verified
CC = Certificate of Completion — closed, HREC
PR = Pending Review — document, status unclear
NFA = No Further Action — closed, HREC if documented

STEERS — State of Texas Environmental Electronic Reporting System:
Manual search required — not available in automated database pulls.
Search URL: https://www2.tceq.texas.gov/oce/eer/index.cfm
Active enforcement = assess seriously, note as data gap pending search
Resolved enforcement = document closure status
Missing STEERS search = MATERIAL DATA GAP — must state impact on REC identification
Regional files: DFW Region 4 Arlington may have files not in statewide system.

IHWCA — Industrial and Hazardous Waste Corrective Action:
Permitted hazardous waste handlers under TCEQ Chapters 335.
Treat like RCRA CORRACTS — presence within 1 mile = Potential REC assessment.
Active IHWCA site within 0.5 mile = strong Potential REC.

VCP — Voluntary Cleanup Program:
Enrollment = Potential REC until Certificate of Completion issued.
Certificate of Completion with no AUL = HREC.
Certificate of Completion with AUL = CREC.
VCP sites indicate known contamination — never downgrade to Proximity Concern.

DRYCLEANER PROGRAM:
TCEQ Dry Cleaner Remediation Program = active or former dry cleaner with release.
Within 0.5 mile = Potential REC — PCE/TCE DNAPLs, VI pathway mandatory.
Former dry cleaner even with closure = assess VI pathway.

## TCEQ CLEANUP STANDARDS — RISK-BASED CORRECTIVE ACTION (RBCA)

Texas uses Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 risk-based cleanup under TCEQ RG-478.

TIER 1 — Generic Lookup Table Standards:
Pre-calculated risk-based concentrations. Most protective. Applied without site-specific data.
Protective of residential receptor unless industrial/commercial land use documented.

TIER 1 RESIDENTIAL SOIL STANDARDS (key contaminants):
Benzene: 0.06 mg/kg
Toluene: 520 mg/kg
Ethylbenzene: 38 mg/kg
Xylene (total): 490 mg/kg
MTBE: 14 mg/kg
Naphthalene: 3.8 mg/kg
PCE (tetrachloroethylene): 0.56 mg/kg
TCE (trichloroethylene): 0.37 mg/kg
Vinyl chloride: 0.014 mg/kg
Lead: 500 mg/kg (TCEQ residential default)
Arsenic: 24 mg/kg (background-based)

TIER 1 COMMERCIAL/INDUSTRIAL SOIL STANDARDS (key contaminants):
Benzene: 1.9 mg/kg
PCE: 6.4 mg/kg
TCE: 4.2 mg/kg
Vinyl chloride: 0.16 mg/kg

GROUNDWATER STANDARDS (TCEQ Protective Concentration Levels):
Benzene: 5 µg/L (= EPA MCL)
Toluene: 1,000 µg/L
Ethylbenzene: 700 µg/L
Xylene: 10,000 µg/L
MTBE: 13 µg/L (TCEQ PCL, more stringent than EPA advisory)
PCE: 5 µg/L (= EPA MCL)
TCE: 5 µg/L (= EPA MCL)
Vinyl chloride: 2 µg/L (= EPA MCL)
Lead: 15 µg/L (= EPA action level)
Arsenic: 10 µg/L (= EPA MCL)

TIER 2 — Site-Specific Risk Assessment:
Uses site-specific data (soil type, depth to groundwater, land use) to derive
alternative cleanup standards. Can be less stringent than Tier 1 for industrial sites.
Requires: receptor survey, exposure pathway analysis, site-specific input parameters.

TIER 3 — Probabilistic Risk Assessment:
Used for complex sites. Requires detailed site characterization.
Rarely used for PST sites — more common for RCRA and industrial sites.

## VAPOR INTRUSION — ASTM E1527-21 REQUIREMENT

Vapor intrusion (VI) became an explicit recognized condition in E1527-21.
VI must be evaluated as a potential pathway in every Phase I.

HIGH VI RISK INDICATORS:
- Former or active dry cleaner within 0.5 mile (PCE/TCE)
- LPST within 0.25 mile with no closure (benzene, MTBE)
- Any chlorinated solvent release within 0.5 mile
- Shallow groundwater (under 20 ft) plus petroleum or chlorinated source

VI ASSESSMENT IN PHASE I:
Must note VI pathway if source within applicable radius.
Must note that VI screening (soil gas, sub-slab sampling) is beyond Phase I scope.
Recommend Phase II ESA with VI component if Potential REC for chlorinated solvents.

TCEQ VAPOR INTRUSION GUIDANCE:
TCEQ VI screening levels (indoor air):
Benzene: 3.7 µg/m³
PCE: 41 µg/m³
TCE: 2.0 µg/m³
Vinyl chloride: 1.1 µg/m³

## PFAS — BEYOND STANDARD SCOPE

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are beyond the scope of ASTM E1527-21.
Must state this explicitly if PFAS concern present.
PFAS sources to note even though beyond scope:
- Military installations (AFFF firefighting foam)
- Commercial airports
- Industrial fire training areas
- Chrome plating operations
- Semiconductor manufacturing
EPA MCL for PFOA: 4 ng/L
EPA MCL for PFOS: 4 ng/L
These are new (2024) — note as emerging regulatory concern.

## MIGRATION PATHWAY ANALYSIS

Upgradient sources: subject property downgradient = HIGH migration risk
Downgradient sources: subject property upgradient = LOW risk unless volatile (VI possible)
Lateral sources: cross-gradient = moderate concern by distance and geology

Depth to groundwater: shallow under 20 ft = higher migration risk
Soil permeability: sand/gravel = faster migration; clay = slower but preferential pathways in cracks
North Texas clay (Houston Black, Burleson): shrink-swell creates vertical cracks — preferential pathways

PLUME DISTANCE GUIDELINES (Texas conditions):
BTEX plumes: typically 100-500 ft downgradient from LPST
MTBE plumes: 500-2000 ft — much more mobile than BTEX
Chlorinated solvent plumes: highly variable, DNAPL sinks, vapor migrates
Within 0.25 mile = strong migration concern
0.25-0.5 mile = moderate concern, assess geology and gradient
Beyond 0.5 mile = migration requires significant evidence to assert as REC

GROUNDWATER GRADIENT — NORTH TEXAS:
General flow: northeast to east across DFW metroplex
Trinity River watershed generally flows SE to Gulf
Elevation data from USGS NED can proxy gradient direction
Field gradient determination requires licensed hydrogeologist

## COMMON ERRORS — FLAG THESE IN REPORT REVIEW

1. Calling proximity a REC without release record
2. Missing closure letter verification for HREC classification
3. Using interim closure as final closure — interim is still a REC
4. Ignoring vapor intrusion pathway for chlorinated solvents
5. Overusing De Minimis for uncharacterized releases
6. Missing TCEQ STEERS manual search — must state as data gap
7. No impact statement for data gaps per ASTM E1527-21 Section 12
8. Stating no contamination found instead of no evidence of release
9. Missing ASTM E1527-21 scope limitation statement
10. Undated database queries — database currency required
11. Calling a screening report a completed Phase I ESA
12. Missing PFAS statement when military or airport present
13. Assigning HREC without verifying final (not interim) closure letter
14. Missing EP qualifications statement
15. Not distinguishing between Potential REC and Proximity Concern
16. Failing to state migration pathway basis for Potential REC classification
17. Using VCP Certificate of Completion as clean — may have AUL restrictions

## EP PROFESSIONAL LIABILITY LANGUAGE

REQUIRED statements in any Phase I ESA:
1. Scope limitation: "This assessment was performed in accordance with ASTM E1527-21
   and does not constitute a guarantee regarding environmental conditions."
2. Data gap impact: Each data gap must be followed by impact statement:
   "This data gap [does/does not] affect the ability to identify RECs."
3. Reliance statement: Results are valid for the user identified in the report.
   Third-party reliance requires reliance letter.
4. Significant data gap vs. practical difficulty:
   Significant data gap: affects ability to identify RECs — must document.
   Practical difficulty: cannot obtain but would not affect conclusions.

LANGUAGE TO AVOID:
- "No contamination found" — say "no evidence of release identified within scope"
- "Clean site" — say "no RECs identified within scope of services performed"
- "Guaranteed" or "certify" — EPs provide professional opinion, not guarantees
- "All databases searched" — specify which databases, dates, radii
- "No further investigation needed" — say "no Phase II ESA recommended at this time"

## TEXAS-SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE

TPDES = Texas NPDES equivalent, administered by TCEQ
Multi-Sector General Permit (MSGP) = industrial stormwater
Construction General Permit (CGP, TXR150000) = construction stormwater
SWPPP required for CGP sites — flag if under construction without SWPPP documentation

VCP: Voluntary Cleanup Program
Enrollment = Potential REC until Certificate of Completion
Certificate of Completion + deed restriction = CREC
Certificate of Completion no restriction = HREC

PST Fund: State fund pays eligible cleanup costs above responsible party deductible.
Fund enrollment DOES NOT mean cleanup is complete or site is closed.
Always check TCEQ PST database for current status, not just fund enrollment.

TCEQ REGIONAL OFFICES — files not always in statewide database:
Region 4 (DFW): Arlington — covers Collin, Dallas, Denton, Tarrant counties
Region 10 (Austin): covers Travis, Williamson, Hays counties
Region 13 (San Antonio): covers Bexar, Comal, Guadalupe counties
Region 14 (Corpus Christi): coastal south Texas

TEXAS RAILROAD COMMISSION (RRC):
Regulates oil and gas wells, pipelines, and associated facilities.
Not under TCEQ — separate database search required for oil and gas sites.
RRC violations = assess as Potential REC for petroleum releases.
Orphan wells on or adjacent to property = flag and assess.

## CONTAMINANT FIELD KNOWLEDGE

BTEX (Benzene, Toluene, Ethylbenzene, Xylene):
Benzene = driver contaminant. MCL 5 µg/L. Carcinogen. Most stringent standard.
BTEX plumes typically 100-500 ft downgradient from LPST source.
Benzene half-life in groundwater 10-730 days depending on conditions.
Aerobic biodegradation occurs — but anaerobic conditions in plume core slow degradation.

MTBE (Methyl tert-butyl ether):
Gasoline additive widely used 1979-2006. Still present in pre-2006 releases.
TCEQ PCL: 13 µg/L — more stringent than EPA advisory (20-40 µg/L).
Very mobile, resistant to biodegradation. Plumes extend 500-2000 ft.
Taste/odor threshold 15-45 µg/L — can cause water supply complaints at sub-health levels.
Flag any LPST opened before 2006 for MTBE concern.

Chlorinated Solvents (PCE, TCE, DCE, VC):
PCE (tetrachloroethylene): dry cleaner solvent. Dense non-aqueous phase liquid (DNAPL).
TCE (trichloroethylene): industrial degreaser. Also DNAPL.
Both sink below water table — difficult to locate, difficult to remediate.
Dechlorination pathway: PCE → TCE → DCE → Vinyl chloride (VC).
Vinyl chloride = most carcinogenic. MCL 2 µg/L. Flag if upgradient dry cleaner.
Former dry cleaner within 0.5 mile = Potential REC regardless of age.
VI pathway mandatory for any chlorinated solvent within 0.5 mile.

Metals:
Lead, arsenic, chromium most common in Phase I contexts.
Lead: typically from lead paint, solder, gasoline additives (pre-1996).
Arsenic: naturally elevated in some Texas soils — background vs. anthropogenic distinction required.
Chromium: electroplating, tanning, wood preservatives — assess if industrial use present.
Hexavalent chromium Cr(VI) > trivalent Cr(III) — toxicity and mobility differ.

PFAS:
Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances. Beyond E1527-21 scope but note if sources present.
AFFF (aqueous film-forming foam): military, airports, industrial fire training.
EPA finalized MCLs in 2024: PFOA 4 ng/L, PFOS 4 ng/L.
Note as emerging regulatory concern if within 1 mile of military or airport.

## CONFIDENCE CALIBRATION

HIGH confidence: multiple corroborating sources, VERIFIED status, standard interpretation,
database current within 90 days, physical site reconnaissance performed.

MEDIUM confidence: single source, INFERRED status, edge case,
data older than 5 years, database currency uncertain.

LOW confidence: UNAVAILABLE status, conflicting sources, manual verification required,
significant data gap present, site recon not performed.

Always state the basis for confidence. Never assign HIGH confidence to inferred data.
Data gaps do not automatically lower REC tier — but must state impact on conclusions.

## STANDARD RECOMMENDATIONS

Site recon not performed: recommend physical site visit before transaction close.
LPST within 0.5 mile no closure: recommend TCEQ PST database verification + site visit.
Active cleanup within 1 mile: recommend Phase II ESA scope discussion.
Former dry cleaner within 0.5 mile: flag VI pathway, recommend Phase II with VI component.
FEMA Zone AE plus regulatory flags: compound risk, flag both constraints together.
STEERS not searched: note as MATERIAL data gap with required impact statement.
Railroad Commission sites adjacent: recommend RRC database search.
PFAS source within 1 mile: note as beyond scope, recommend PFAS assessment.
Former industrial use with no regulatory record: flag historical gap, recommend Phase II.
