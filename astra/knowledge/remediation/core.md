## PHASE II ESA — SCOPE AND TRIGGERS

Phase II ESA is a site investigation to confirm or deny RECs identified in Phase I.
Standard scope: soil sampling + groundwater monitoring well installation + lab analysis.

MANDATORY Phase II triggers (never recommend against Phase II for these):
- Known release on-site with no documented closure
- Active LPST within 0.25 mile no closure letter
- Former dry cleaner on-site or within 0.25 mile
- Field evidence of release (staining, odors, UST/AST evidence, drums)
- Active cleanup site within 0.25 mile of subject property
- RCRA CORRACTS site within 0.5 mile

RECOMMENDED Phase II triggers:
- LPST within 0.5 mile no closure
- Multiple LPST sites within 1 mile (density concern)
- Former gas station or auto repair on-site (historical use)
- Former industrial use on-site without regulatory record
- FEMA Zone AE plus regulatory flags (compound risk)
- Dry cleaner within 0.5 mile (VI pathway)
- Unexplained soil staining visible in aerial imagery

Phase II NOT recommended when:
- All regulatory listings have documented final closure
- No field evidence of release
- No historical high-risk use
- Regulatory listings are permitted facilities with no violations (Proximity Concern only)

## PHASE II SCOPE DESIGN

STANDARD SCOPE (petroleum sites — LPST, PST):
Minimum 3 soil borings: one at source area, two in suspected plume direction
Soil sampling: continuous from surface to water table at 2-5 ft intervals
Groundwater: minimum 3 monitoring wells forming triangle around suspected source
Lab analysis: BTEX (benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene, xylene), TPH-GRO, TPH-DRO, MTBE
Report: boring logs, well construction, analytical tables, plume maps, receptor evaluation

EXPANDED SCOPE (chlorinated solvents — PCE/TCE):
Deeper borings to saturated zone and below
Additional parameters: PCE, TCE, DCE isomers (cis-1,2-DCE most common), vinyl chloride
Vapor intrusion component: soil gas probes, sub-slab sampling
Dense NAPL (DNAPL) investigation below water table
Additional wells to characterize vertical distribution

METALS SCOPE (industrial, mining, historical):
XRF screening in field — rapid screening for lead, arsenic, chromium, zinc
Confirmatory lab: TCLP (toxicity characteristic leaching procedure) for waste classification
Soil parameters: SW-846 Methods 3050B/6010 for metals
Groundwater: dissolved metals, pH, Eh, alkalinity

ASBESTOS/LEAD SCOPE (building demolition, renovation):
Asbestos: AHERA-compliant bulk sampling by accredited inspector
Lead paint: XRF screening or chip sampling
ACM in soil from demolition: TCLP if disposal required

## TCEQ CLEANUP PROGRAMS

### PST (Petroleum Storage Tank) Program
Governs LPST releases. Most common cleanup program in Texas.
Administered under TCEQ Chapter 334.

PST FUND:
State fund reimbursing eligible cleanup costs above responsible party deductible.
Deductible: $5,000-$10,000 depending on tank size and owner type.
Fund pays 100% of eligible costs above deductible up to fund limits.
Fund enrollment ≠ cleanup complete. Check current status in TCEQ PST database.
NOT all costs eligible: legal fees, duplicate work, work without TCEQ approval not reimbursed.

PST CLEANUP PROCESS:
1. Release confirmed → notify TCEQ within 24 hours (TCEQ Rule 334.72)
2. Initial abatement: stop release, remove free product
3. Site assessment: characterize extent of contamination
4. Corrective Action Plan (CAP): submit to TCEQ for approval
5. Corrective action implementation
6. Interim closure (IC): work ongoing but documented
7. Final closure (FC): TCEQ verifies cleanup meets standards
8. Certificate of Completion (CC): formal closure document
CRITICAL: Only FC and CC indicate actual closure. IC = still active site, still a REC.

PST STATUS DEFINITIONS:
Open/Active: Release confirmed, cleanup ongoing or not yet started → REC
Interim Closure: Progress documented, some work done, not fully closed → REC
Final Closure: TCEQ verified cleanup met standards → HREC (verify letter)
Certificate of Completion: Formal TCEQ document → HREC or CREC if AUL present
No Further Action (NFA): Contamination below action levels → HREC

### VCP — Voluntary Cleanup Program
TCEQ Chapter 361 — for brownfields and non-PST contaminated sites.
Enrollment voluntary but provides TCEQ oversight and liability protection.
Certificate of Completion = closure document.
Key benefit: liability protection for innocent landowners and lenders.
VCP sites often have deed restrictions (AULs) — check county records.

### DRY CLEANER REMEDIATION PROGRAM
TCEQ Chapter 374 — PCE/TCE releases from dry cleaning facilities.
State fund pays cleanup costs for eligible releases.
Active enrollment = release confirmed, cleanup ongoing → REC
Certificate of Completion = closure → HREC or CREC

### IHWCA — Industrial and Hazardous Waste Corrective Action
TCEQ Chapter 335 — RCRA equivalent for industrial facilities.
Active IHWCA site = significant contamination → treat as REC
Corrective Measures Implementation (CMI) = remediation underway

## CONTAMINANT BEHAVIOR — FATE AND TRANSPORT

### Petroleum Hydrocarbons (BTEX + TPH)

LNAPL (Light Non-Aqueous Phase Liquid): petroleum floats on water table.
Release pathway: UST leak → vadose zone migration → water table → LNAPL lens.
LNAPL recovery required before dissolved plume cleanup effective.

BTEX dissolved plume:
Benzene = driver contaminant (MCL 5 µg/L, carcinogen, most mobile).
Toluene, ethylbenzene, xylene biodegrade faster than benzene under aerobic conditions.
Anaerobic core of plume: slower biodegradation, benzene persists longest.
Typical BTEX plume: 100-500 ft downgradient from LPST source.
Monitoring period: typically 3-5 years after LNAPL removal to confirm stability.

MTBE:
Highly mobile, resistant to biodegradation. TCEQ PCL: 13 µg/L.
Plume can extend 500-2,000 ft from source.
Not effectively remediated by standard biosparging — requires different approach.
Flavor/odor threshold: 15-45 µg/L — causes water supply complaints below health threshold.
Flag all LPST opened before 2006 for MTBE.

TPH (Total Petroleum Hydrocarbons):
TPH-GRO (gasoline range organics): lighter fractions, more volatile, more mobile.
TPH-DRO (diesel range organics): heavier fractions, less mobile, more persistent.
TPH alone not cleanup standard — individual BTEX constituents drive standards.

### Chlorinated Solvents (PCE, TCE, DCE, VC)

DNAPL (Dense Non-Aqueous Phase Liquid): sinks below water table.
PCE and TCE are DNAPLs — migrate downward through aquifer.
DNAPL source zones are very difficult to locate and remediate.
Standard monitoring wells may miss DNAPL pools sitting in aquifer lows.

REDUCTIVE DECHLORINATION PATHWAY:
PCE → TCE → cis-1,2-DCE → vinyl chloride (VC) → ethene (complete)
This pathway occurs naturally under anaerobic conditions.
Vinyl chloride is the end product — most carcinogenic — MCL 2 µg/L.
CRITICAL: If PCE or TCE present, always analyze for VC.
Presence of cis-1,2-DCE without VC = incomplete dechlorination, VC may form over time.
Enhanced reductive dechlorination (ERD) can be stimulated with electron donor injection.

VAPOR INTRUSION:
Chlorinated solvents are volatile — VI pathway mandatory for any PCE/TCE site.
PCE/TCE in groundwater → volatile in vadose zone → intrudes through foundation.
Buildings directly over or downgradient from source = VI investigation required.
Sub-slab soil gas > 1 µg/m³ TCE = potential VI concern.
Indoor air TCE > 2.0 µg/m³ = TCEQ VI screening level exceeded.

### Metals

Lead:
Common at former firing ranges, battery recycling, smelters, gasoline additive (pre-1996).
Lead in soil is relatively immobile — stays near source.
TCEQ residential soil: 500 mg/kg. Industrial: 1500 mg/kg.
Lead in groundwater: 15 µg/L EPA action level.
Bioavailability testing (RBALP) can reduce cleanup obligations if applicable.

Arsenic:
Naturally elevated in some Texas soils — background vs. anthropogenic distinction required.
Texas background arsenic: typically 5-15 mg/kg.
TCEQ residential soil standard: 24 mg/kg (background-based).
Anthropogenic sources: pesticides (orchards, cotton fields), wood preservatives (CCA), mining.
Historic cotton fields in Texas: arsenic from pesticide application — common finding.

Chromium:
Hexavalent Cr(VI) vs. trivalent Cr(III) — vastly different toxicity.
Cr(VI): carcinogen, highly mobile. Cr(III): low toxicity, less mobile.
Electroplating, tanning, wood preservatives (CCA) are sources.
Speciation analysis required if chromium present — don't treat total chromium as Cr(VI).

### PFAS

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances — beyond ASTM E1527-21 scope but note sources.
AFFF foam: military bases, airports, industrial fire training = primary sources.
EPA MCLs finalized 2024: PFOA 4 ng/L, PFOS 4 ng/L — among most stringent ever.
Remediation technology: ion exchange, granular activated carbon (GAC) for groundwater.
No proven in-situ destruction technology exists yet — destruction under research.
Regulatory framework evolving rapidly — monitor TCEQ and EPA guidance.

## RISK-BASED CLEANUP STANDARDS

Texas uses Tier 1/2/3 RBCA under TCEQ RG-478.
Cleanup standards are land-use dependent — residential more stringent than industrial.

TIER 1 KEY SOIL STANDARDS (residential):
Benzene: 0.06 mg/kg
PCE: 0.56 mg/kg
TCE: 0.37 mg/kg
Vinyl chloride: 0.014 mg/kg
Lead: 500 mg/kg
Arsenic: 24 mg/kg
MTBE: 14 mg/kg

TIER 1 GROUNDWATER STANDARDS (Protective Concentration Levels):
Benzene: 5 µg/L
PCE: 5 µg/L
TCE: 5 µg/L
Vinyl chloride: 2 µg/L
MTBE: 13 µg/L
Lead: 15 µg/L
Arsenic: 10 µg/L

LAND USE IMPACT ON STANDARDS:
Residential standards apply unless industrial/commercial land use documented.
Industrial/commercial standards can be 10-100x less stringent for carcinogens.
Deed restrictions to industrial use reduce cleanup costs significantly.
AUL (Activity and Use Limitation) required if residual contamination left in place.
Receptor survey: if no sensitive receptors within plume area, risk-based argument stronger.

## REMEDIATION TECHNOLOGY SELECTION

### Petroleum — Soil
Excavation and disposal: most definitive for shallow contamination (<10 ft).
Limit: typically cost-effective up to 2,000 tons or accessible depths.
Biopile: ex-situ bioremediation for large volumes.
Land treatment: spreading in thin lifts, effective for TPH.
In-situ bioremediation (ISB): oxygen or nutrient injection to stimulate aerobic degradation.
Air sparging/SVE (soil vapor extraction): effective for volatile BTEX in vadose zone.
Multi-phase extraction: combined LNAPL + vapor + groundwater extraction.

### Petroleum — Groundwater
Pump and treat (P&T): extract contaminated groundwater, treat above ground.
  Standard approach but can take decades for dissolved plume.
  Required for LNAPL recovery before P&T effective for dissolved phase.
Permeable reactive barriers (PRB): in-situ treatment wall.
Monitored natural attenuation (MNA): document natural biodegradation.
  MNA appropriate when: stable or shrinking plume, no sensitive receptors, benzene declining.
  MNA requires: multiple years of monitoring data, demonstrated degradation.
  MNA not appropriate: active LNAPL, mobile plume, sensitive receptor within plume path.

### Chlorinated Solvents — Groundwater
Enhanced reductive dechlorination (ERD): inject electron donor (molasses, EVO, lactate).
  Stimulates anaerobic bacteria to dechlorinate PCE/TCE → ethene.
  Must monitor VC — VC spike expected before complete dechlorination.
  Takes 2-5 years for significant reduction.
In-situ chemical oxidation (ISCO): inject oxidant (permanganate, persulfate, Fenton's).
  Rapid treatment but DNAPL source zones difficult to reach.
  Rebound common if DNAPL not fully contacted.
Thermal treatment (ERH/steam): heat subsurface to volatilize DNAPL.
  Most effective for DNAPL source zones.
  High cost — typically $500K-$2M+ for commercial site.
P&T with GAC: standard for dissolved plume management.
  Not effective for DNAPL source zones — long-term operation required.
Phytoremediation: trees transpire groundwater, uptake some contaminants.
  Long time horizon (decades), low cost, effective for large diffuse plumes.

### Metals — Soil
Excavation: definitive but expensive for large volumes.
Soil washing: separate contaminated fines from clean coarse particles.
Stabilization/solidification: immobilize metals in place — reduces leachability.
Phytoremediation: some plants accumulate metals (hyperaccumulators).
  Indian mustard for lead, sunflowers for cesium — limited scale.
Cap and AUL: cover contamination, restrict land use — not cleanup, just control.

## CLOSURE DOCUMENTATION — WHAT TO LOOK FOR

FINAL CLOSURE LETTER must contain:
- TCEQ case number and site address
- Statement that corrective action is complete
- Confirmation cleanup standards met
- Effective date of closure
- Any AULs or deed restrictions required

WATCH FOR:
Interim vs. final closure — interim means work ongoing, not closed.
Conditional closure — may require monitoring or land use restriction.
AUL in closure — requires deed restriction recorded in county records.
Third-party closure letters — some states accept consultant letters; Texas requires TCEQ sign-off.
Closure letter date vs. database status — database may not reflect recent closure.

HREC DOCUMENTATION CHECKLIST:
☐ Final closure letter (not interim)
☐ TCEQ database status = FC or CC
☐ No AULs or deed restrictions (otherwise CREC)
☐ Closure letter signed by TCEQ (not just consultant)
☐ Closure date and case number documented in report

## COST ESTIMATION — PHASE II AND REMEDIATION

PHASE II ESA COST RANGES (Texas, 2026):
Standard 3-well petroleum investigation: $15,000-$35,000
Full 5-well investigation with VI component: $35,000-$75,000
Complex multi-source investigation: $75,000-$200,000+

REMEDIATION COST RANGES (Texas):
Petroleum soil excavation (shallow, small volume): $25,000-$100,000
Petroleum P&T system (3-5 years): $150,000-$500,000
BTEX MNA monitoring program (5 years): $50,000-$150,000
PCE/TCE ERD treatment: $200,000-$800,000
PCE/TCE thermal treatment (DNAPL source): $500,000-$2,000,000+
Metals soil remediation (excavation): $100,000-$500,000+

DEAL IMPACT GUIDANCE:
Low risk (no RECs, no regulatory flags): minimal liability, no Phase II
Moderate risk (proximity concerns, older LPST): budget $25K-$75K for Phase II
Elevated risk (potential REC, open LPST): budget $50K-$200K Phase II + potential remediation
High risk (confirmed REC, active cleanup): budget $200K-$1M+ remediation
Dry cleaner on-site: budget $500K-$2M+ PCE/TCE remediation
Former industrial on-site: scope dependent, can exceed $5M

## COMMON ERRORS IN REMEDIATION ASSESSMENT

1. Calling interim closure a clean site — IC is still active, still a REC
2. Missing vinyl chloride — always analyze for VC when PCE/TCE present
3. Ignoring DNAPL source zone — single groundwater well misses DNAPL pools
4. Underestimating MTBE plume — travels much farther than BTEX
5. Accepting MNA without adequate monitoring data
6. Missing LNAPL recovery requirement before dissolved phase remediation
7. Not checking for AULs/deed restrictions on closed sites
8. Confusing PST Fund enrollment with cleanup completion
9. Missing VI pathway for any chlorinated solvent site
10. Using residential cleanup standards for industrial properties (overly conservative)
11. Accepting consultant closure letter without TCEQ sign-off as HREC
12. Not flagging pre-2006 LPST sites for MTBE
13. Missing reductive dechlorination intermediates (DCE, VC) in chlorinated solvent analysis
14. Underestimating cost of DNAPL remediation in Phase I liability estimate
