## PHASE II ESA — SCOPE AND TRIGGERS

Phase II ESA is a site investigation to confirm or deny RECs identified in Phase I.
Standard scope includes soil sampling and groundwater monitoring well installation.

MANDATORY Phase II triggers:
- Known release on-site with no documented closure
- Former dry cleaner on or adjacent — chlorinated solvents
- Former gas station on-site — UST petroleum
- Active cleanup site within 0.25 mile downgradient
- LPST within 0.25 mile with open status
- Field evidence: staining, odors, UST presence, drums

RECOMMENDED Phase II triggers:
- LPST within 0.5 mile downgradient with no closure
- Former industrial use with unknown chemical history
- High CETO Score (below 55) with multiple regulatory flags
- Lender or investor requiring environmental clearance
- Acquisition of commercial or industrial property

NOT a Phase II trigger alone:
- Proximity to facility with no release record (Proximity Concern)
- NPDES permit presence without violations
- TRI reporting facility without release evidence near site

## TCEQ CLEANUP PROGRAMS

### PST (Petroleum Storage Tank) Program
Governs LPST releases. Most common cleanup program in Texas.

PST Fund: State fund reimbursing eligible cleanup costs.
- Fund pays after responsible party pays deductible ($5K-$10K)
- Fund pays up to $1M per release
- Releases before 1989 may have different coverage

Cleanup stages:
1. Initial Abatement: Stop the source, remove free product
2. Site Assessment: Define extent of contamination
3. Risk Assessment: Determine cleanup standards needed
4. Corrective Action: Implement cleanup
5. Closure: Document completion

Closure types:
- No Further Action (NFA): Cleanup complete, no restrictions
- Certificate of Completion with deed restriction: Residual contamination
  remains but controlled — CREC classification
- Interim closure: Monitoring ongoing — still a REC

CRITICAL: Interim closure ≠ final closure.
Always verify closure type when assessing LPST sites.
A site showing "closed" in TCEQ database may have interim closure only.

### VCP (Voluntary Cleanup Program)
For brownfields and sites not in PST Fund.
Applicant-driven — owner or prospective purchaser applies.
TCEQ oversees and approves cleanup.

VCP stages:
- Application accepted: REC — active investigation pending
- Investigation complete: Still REC until Certificate of Completion
- Certificate of Completion (COC): HREC if no restrictions, CREC if restrictions
- COC with Affected Property Assessment: Site assessed but not cleaned up
  This is a critical nuance — COC can be issued for risk-based closure
  where contamination remains but risk is managed institutionally

### Dry Cleaner Remediation Program
State-funded cleanup for dry cleaner releases.
Similar structure to PST Fund but for PCE/TCE releases.
Contaminated sites are enrolled and funded for cleanup.

Chlorinated solvent behavior (PCE/TCE):
- Dense Non-Aqueous Phase Liquid (DNAPL) — sinks below water table
- Forms source zone in saturated zone
- Daughter products: PCE → TCE → DCE → vinyl chloride (VC)
- Vinyl chloride is a known carcinogen, most mobile, lowest MCL
- DNAPL source zones are extremely difficult to remediate
- Natural attenuation often the only feasible long-term approach

Phase II scope for dry cleaner:
- Soil vapor survey first (PCE/TCE volatile — SV efficient screening)
- Soil borings in source area (former still room, separator)
- Groundwater monitoring wells downgradient
- Must assess vapor intrusion pathway — VI assessment required

### IHWCA (Industrial and Hazardous Waste Compliance Assessment)
For industrial facilities with hazardous waste violations.
TCEQ enforcement-driven, not voluntary.

## CONTAMINANT BEHAVIOR — FIELD KNOWLEDGE

### Petroleum Hydrocarbons (BTEX + TPH)
Source: LPST releases, above-ground tank spills, pipeline leaks.
Behavior:
- LNAPL (Light Non-Aqueous Phase Liquid) — floats on water table
- BTEX is the regulatory driver — benzene at 5 ppb MCL in groundwater
- Free product (LNAPL) creates ongoing source — must be removed
- Dissolved phase plume extends downgradient
- Natural attenuation (biodegradation) effective for BTEX
- Typical plume length in Texas: 100-500 ft for BTEX, longer for MTBE

Texas groundwater cleanup standards (PST):
- Benzene: 5 ppb (MCL) or risk-based
- TPH-GRO: 1,400 ppb (risk-based, receptor-dependent)
- MTBE: 13 ppb (Texas standard, stricter than EPA)

### Chlorinated Solvents (PCE, TCE, DCE, VC)
Source: Dry cleaners, industrial degreasers, metal plating.
Behavior:
- DNAPL — denser than water, sinks to bottom of aquifer
- Creates persistent source zone that slowly dissolves
- Sequential reductive dechlorination produces daughter products
- Most sites have mixture of PCE + TCE + cis-1,2-DCE + VC
- VC is most mobile and most toxic — always report if detected

Texas groundwater standards:
- PCE: 5 ppb (MCL)
- TCE: 5 ppb (MCL)
- cis-1,2-DCE: 70 ppb (MCL)
- Vinyl chloride: 2 ppb (MCL) — often the limiting constituent

Remediation options:
- In-situ chemical reduction (ISCR): Inject reductants (ZVI, sulfate reducers)
- Enhanced reductive dechlorination (ERD): Inject electron donors
- Soil vapor extraction (SVE): For vadose zone
- Multi-phase extraction (MPE): Combines groundwater and vapor extraction
- Monitored natural attenuation (MNA): Often necessary long-term
- Thermal treatment (ISTR): For high concentration source zones

### Heavy Metals
Source: Industrial operations, mining, smelting, electroplating.
Behavior:
- Unlike organics, metals do not biodegrade
- Sorption to soil particles limits mobility in some cases
- pH controls mobility — acidic conditions increase mobility
- Lead most common at firing ranges, battery facilities
- Arsenic naturally elevated in some Texas soils (background issue)
- Chromium VI (hexavalent) — carcinogen, highly mobile in groundwater

Texas background arsenic: Varies by county — can exceed MCL naturally.
Always establish background before attributing arsenic to site.

### PFAS (Per- and Polyfluoroalkyl Substances)
Source: AFFF firefighting foam, industrial processes, consumer products.
Beyond standard Phase I scope (ASTM E1527-21 does not require PFAS).
BUT: EPA MCL established 2024 — 4 ppt for PFOA, 4 ppt for PFOS.
Sites with AFFF use (airports, military, fire training areas) = flag for PFAS.
Behavior: Highly mobile, very persistent, bioaccumulates.
This is an emerging issue that will increasingly appear in due diligence.

## RISK-BASED CLEANUP STANDARDS

Texas uses risk-based corrective action (RBCA) for most sites.
Cleanup standards are not one-size-fits-all — they depend on:

1. Land use (residential vs commercial/industrial)
2. Exposure pathways (ingestion, inhalation, dermal)
3. Receptor locations (schools, daycare, residences nearby)
4. Groundwater use (drinking water aquifer vs non-use)

Residential standards are MORE stringent than commercial.
A site cleaned up to commercial standards has deed restrictions prohibiting
residential use — this is a CREC, not an HREC.

Key exposure pathways for Texas:
- Direct contact with contaminated soil (construction workers, children)
- Vapor intrusion (volatile organics migrating into buildings)
- Groundwater ingestion (if aquifer is a drinking water source)
- Surface water impact (if contamination reaches waterway)

## VAPOR INTRUSION — CRITICAL PATHWAY

Vapor intrusion (VI) is the migration of volatile chemicals from soil or
groundwater into overlying buildings through foundation gaps.

ASTM E1527-21 requires VI to be considered in Phase I.

High VI risk indicators:
- Chlorinated solvents within 100 feet of building
- Petroleum hydrocarbons with BTEX within 30 feet of building
- Shallow contamination (<30 feet to contamination source)
- Building with basement or slab-on-grade (no barrier)
- Active LPST or dry cleaner site within 100 meters

Texas VI guidance: TCEQ has vapor intrusion guidance based on EPA methods.
VI pathway assessment may require indoor air sampling.
This is often the controlling exposure pathway for redevelopment sites.

## REMEDIATION TECHNOLOGY SELECTION

Quick reference for common Texas scenarios:

LPST shallow petroleum in soil:
→ Excavation and disposal (if <2,000 tons and accessible)
→ Biopile or landfarming for large volumes
→ In-situ bioventing for vadose zone

LPST petroleum in groundwater (free product):
→ Product recovery (skimming wells, trenches)
→ Air sparging + SVE combination
→ Monitored natural attenuation after free product removal

Dry cleaner DNAPL source:
→ In-situ thermal treatment (ISTR) for source zone
→ ERD injection for dissolved phase plume
→ SVE for vadose zone PCE
→ Long-term MNA with VC monitoring

Heavy metals in soil:
→ Excavation and disposal
→ Stabilization/solidification
→ Capping (institutional control)

## COMMON ERRORS IN REMEDIATION ASSESSMENT

1. Calling interim closure a clean site: Interim closure = REC, not HREC.
2. Missing vinyl chloride: Always check for VC when PCE/TCE present.
3. Ignoring DNAPL source zone: Single groundwater well may miss DNAPL.
4. Underestimating MTBE plume: MTBE travels much farther than BTEX.
5. Missing VI pathway: Required by ASTM E1527-21 for volatile contaminants.
6. Assuming risk-based closure = safe for residential: Check deed restrictions.
7. Missing PFAS at AFFF sites: Not in standard Phase I scope but growing issue.
8. Ignoring background arsenic: Texas soils have variable natural arsenic.
