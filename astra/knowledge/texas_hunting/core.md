## TEXAS ECOREGIONS — HUNTING ZONES

### 1. Pineywoods (East Texas)
Counties: Sabine, San Augustine, Shelby, Nacogdoches, Angelina, Jasper, Newton,
  Tyler, Hardin, Orange, Jefferson, Polk, Trinity, Houston, Cherokee, Rusk,
  Panola, Harrison, Marion, Cass, Morris, Upshur, Gregg, Smith, Wood, Titus,
  Camp, Franklin, Red River, Bowie
Terrain: Dense pine-hardwood forest, river bottoms, swamps, creek drainages
Soils: Sandy, acidic, well-drained uplands; hydric bottomlands
Key species: White-tailed deer (East Texas strain, smaller body), wild turkey
  (Eastern subspecies), squirrel (fox and gray), feral hog, alligator,
  waterfowl (wood duck, mallard, teal), woodcock, snipe, rabbit, raccoon,
  coyote, bobcat

### 2. Post Oak Savanna

Key species: Feral hog, white-tailed deer (creek bottoms), dove (mourning),

Key species: White-tailed deer (Edwards Plateau strain — BEST deer hunting in

  exotic species with NO closed season and NO bag limit on private land in Texas.

### 8. South Texas Plains (Tamaulipan Thornscrub)

### 9. Gulf Coast Prairies & Marshes
Counties: Orange, Jefferson, Chambers, Galveston, Brazoria, Matagorda,

  Kleberg (coastal), Kenedy (coastal), Willacy (coastal), Cameron (coastal)

  alligator, feral hog, dove, snipe, rail, gallinule, coyote, bobcat,
  nutria, raccoon, mink

  numbers among highest in North America. Sea ducks in winter.

### 10. Trans-Pecos (West Texas / Big Bend Country)
Counties: El Paso, Hudspeth, Culberson, Jeff Davis, Presidio, Brewster,
  Pecos, Terrell (western), Val Verde (western), Reeves, Ward, Winkler,
  Loving, Ector, Midland, Crane, Upton, Crockett
Terrain: Chihuahuan Desert, mountain ranges, bajadas, playas, river canyons
Key species: Mule deer (best in Texas — Hudspeth, Culberson, Jeff Davis),

  black bear (Big Bend area — protected in state parks, huntable on some
  private land with permit)

## TEXAS GAME SPECIES — SEASONS & REGULATIONS

### White-tailed Deer
General season: Late October through early January (exact dates by zone)
Special youth season: Mid-October (one weekend)
Antlerless season: Varies by county — check TPWD county regulations
Bag limit: 5 deer total, no more than 2 bucks (with antler restrictions in
  some counties)
Antler restrictions: Many counties require at least 1 unbranched antler
  OR 13-inch inside spread — check county-specific regs
Archery: September 28 through November 1 statewide + general season
Muzzleloader: Late January (after general season) — limited counties
License required: Hunting license + deer tag (2 included with license)
MLDP (Managed Lands Deer Program): Extended seasons and special provisions
  for landowners who meet TPWD habitat management criteria

### Mule Deer
Season: November–December in Trans-Pecos and Panhandle zones
Bag limit: 1 buck (antler restrictions apply — minimum 3 points on one side
  OR 20-inch outside spread in most areas)
License: Hunting license + mule deer tag (separate from whitetail tag)
Primary zones: Trans-Pecos (Hudspeth, Culberson, Jeff Davis, Presidio,
  Brewster, Pecos counties) and High Plains/Rolling Plains

### Pronghorn
Season: Late September to early October — EXTREMELY limited
Method: Controlled by county — some counties require drawn permit
Bag limit: 1
Primary counties: Presidio, Brewster, Jeff Davis, Pecos, Terrell, Val Verde,
  Reeves, Ward, Andrews, Gaines, Yoakum, Terry, Lynn, Crosby, Garza (Panhandle)
Note: Pronghorn numbers have declined — check current TPWD regulations carefully

### Wild Turkey
Rio Grande subspecies: Most common — Hill Country, South Texas, Rolling Plains,
  Cross Timbers, Panhandle, Trans-Pecos
Eastern subspecies: East Texas Pineywoods only
Merriam's subspecies: Trans-Pecos mountains only
Spring season: April (varies by zone) — TOMS ONLY
Fall season: October–January (varies by zone) — either sex
Bag limit: Spring: 4 per season. Fall: 5 per season (1/day)
License: Hunting license — no separate tag required

### Feral Hog
NO closed season. NO bag limit. NO license required to hunt on private land
  (license required on public land)
Methods: Any legal firearm, archery, air gun, any time day or night
Trapping: Legal and encouraged
Aerial: Legal with landowner permission and aerial hunting permit (TPWD)
Note: Texas has ~2.6 million feral hogs — largest population in US.
  Considered invasive pest. TPWD actively encourages eradication.

### Javelina (Collared Peccary)
Range: South Texas Plains and Trans-Pecos
Season: Open year-round in most of range (check county regs)
Bag limit: 2 per license year
License: Hunting license required
Note: Javelina are NOT pigs — they are a separate species (Tayassuidae family)

### Dove
Mourning Dove:
  North Zone: September 1 – November 14 and December 20 – January 3
  Central Zone: September 1 – November 7 and December 20 – January 3
  South Zone: September 14 – November 7 and December 20 – January 3
Bag limit: 15/day mourning + white-winged combined; 2 white-tipped
White-winged Dove:
  Special early season: First two weekends of September (Friday PM–Sunday)
  South Zone opens first — best hunting
  Primary areas: Lower Rio Grande Valley, South Texas, Trans-Pecos (El Paso)
License: Hunting license + federal migratory bird stamp if not exempt by age

### Quail
Bobwhite quail: October 27 – February 23 (statewide, varies by zone)
Scaled (blue) quail: Same season — Trans-Pecos and Panhandle
Gambel's quail: Trans-Pecos only — same season window
Bag limit: 15/day bobwhite; 15/day scaled; combined limit in overlap zones
Best populations: South Texas (Webb, Zapata, Duval, LaSalle, McMullen counties)
  and Rolling Plains/High Plains for scaled quail
Note: Bobwhite populations have declined 85% since 1980 — habitat loss primary driver

### Pheasant (Ring-necked)
Season: December (first Saturday) through January 1
Zone: Panhandle counties only (Dallam, Sherman, Hansford, Moore, Roberts,
  Ochiltree, Lipscomb, Hartley, Hutchinson, Hemphill, Gray, Wheeler, Carson)
Bag limit: 3 roosters/day; 12 possession
License: Hunting license + TPWD pheasant stamp ($7)
Note: Texas pheasant population variable — best years follow wet springs

### Sandhill Crane
Season: November – February (three zones — Panhandle, North, South)
Panhandle Zone: Opens late October
Bag limit: 3/day
Federal duck stamp required + state hunting license
Best areas: High Plains playa lakes (Lubbock, Crosby, Lynn counties)
  and Rolling Plains (Childress, Hall, Collingsworth counties)
Note: Greater sandhill cranes (larger) winter in massive numbers in Panhandle.
  Lesser sandhills mixed in. Whooping cranes (endangered) migrate through —
  NEVER legal to shoot.

### Waterfowl (Ducks & Geese)
Seasons set annually by US Fish & Wildlife Service + TPWD
Texas has two zones: High Plains Mallard Management Unit and Regular Zone
Duck season: Generally November through January (60 days)
Bag limit: 6 ducks/day (species-specific limits within — pintail 1/day,
  canvasback 1/day, redhead 3/day, scaup 1/day currently)
Light geese (snow, Ross's, blue): Conservation Order — January through March —
  NO bag limit, electronic calls legal, unplugged guns legal
Dark geese (Canada, white-fronted/specklebelly): Separate season and bag limit
Federal duck stamp required ($25) + state license + HIP registration
Best areas: Gulf Coast marshes (best in state), coastal bays, High Plains
  playas, East Texas river bottoms and reservoirs, rice fields (Matagorda/
  Jackson/Victoria counties)
Mottled duck: Texas nesting species — restricted limits

### Alligator
Season: September (archery) + general season — by permit
Method: Hook and line, snare, archery, firearms
Permit: Core counties require Alligator Hunting License ($12)
Non-core counties: License not required but must follow regulations
Range: East Texas east of I-35 generally; Gulf Coast; some Hill Country rivers
Note: American alligator is recovered — no longer listed. Texas has ~500,000.

## PRIVATE LAND ACCESS — LEGAL FRAMEWORK

### Trespass Law
Texas Penal Code § 30.05: Criminal trespass on agricultural land.
  Felony if hunter refuses to leave after notice.
  Notice = verbal, written, posted signs, purple paint (Texas law).
Purple Paint Rule: Purple paint marks on trees/posts = no trespassing.
  Paint must be: vertical lines 8+ inches long, 1+ inch wide, 3-5 feet
  from ground, spaced no more than 100 feet apart in forested areas,
  1,000 feet apart in open country.

### Landowner Liability
Texas Recreation Use Statute: Landowners who allow free public access
  for recreation have LIMITED liability. Landowners who CHARGE for access
  lose this protection.
Agricultural Exemption: Land in ag use qualifies for reduced property taxes.

### TPWD Public Hunting Program
Type I: Walk-in public access (APH permit required)
Type II: Quota hunts — drawn permits, limited hunters
Type III: Lottery/special drawn hunts for trophy management units
MLDP leases: Managed Lands Deer Program — extended seasons for cooperators

## EXOTIC SPECIES — UNIQUE TO TEXAS

Texas has the world's largest private game ranching industry.
These exotic (non-native) species are considered livestock under Texas law
when confined. On high-fenced ranches:
- NO closed season
- NO bag limit
- NO license required (hunting license still recommended)

### Common Exotics in Texas
Axis Deer (Chital): Hill Country dominant. Year-round. Most common exotic.
  Excellent table fare. Antlers typically in velvet spring/summer.
Blackbuck Antelope: South Texas and Hill Country. Fastest Texas antelope.
Aoudad (Barbary Sheep): Trans-Pecos and Hill Country. VERY difficult hunt.
  Wild free-ranging populations in Palo Duro Canyon (legal, needs permit),
  Trans-Pecos mountains, and some Hill Country ranches.
Fallow Deer: Hill Country ranches. Multiple color phases.
Nilgai Antelope: South Texas coastal — King Ranch and surrounding ranches.
  Largest antelope in Texas. Semi-free ranging in South Texas.
  Some public opportunities on Laguna Atascosa NWR.
Sika Deer: Hill Country ranches. Japanese origin.
Scimitar-horned Oryx: Edwards Plateau — once extinct in wild, Texas ranches
  have largest population in world. TPWD manages reintroduction program.
Addax: Edwards Plateau ranches.
Greater Kudu: Some Hill Country operations.
Elk (Rocky Mountain): Free-ranging populations in Trans-Pecos (Guadalupe
  Mountains area, Davis Mountains). TPWD manages — special permit required.
Desert Bighorn Sheep: Reintroduced — Trans-Pecos. Highly regulated.
  Permits are raffle/auction — can cost $50,000+. Population recovering.

## MIGRATORY BIRD ZONES

### Central Flyway
Primary flyway for Texas interior — waterfowl, doves, shorebirds, cranes.
Most of Texas falls in Central Flyway.

### Mississippi Flyway
Overlaps in East Texas — Jefferson, Orange, Hardin, Jasper counties (coastal).
Some ducks follow Mississippi Flyway down coastal corridor.

### Key Migration Corridors
Playa Lakes (High Plains): 19,000+ playas — critically important for
  75% of Central Flyway waterfowl. Irrigation depletion major threat.
Coastal Prairie (Gulf): Snow geese winter — largest concentrations in world.
  Light goose conservation order allows extended hunting.
Texas Bottleneck: Hawk migration through Corpus Christi/Kingsville area
  (not legal to hunt — protected raptors).

## COUNTY-SPECIFIC DEER REGULATIONS

Texas deer regulations vary significantly by county.
Key classifications:
- Antler restriction counties (mandatory minimums)
- MLD (Managed Lands Deer) cooperator counties (extended seasons)
- Antlerless permit counties (additional does require special permit)
- Trap-Neuter-Relocate prohibited counties

High-density counties (most deer per square mile):
Kleberg, Kenedy, Brooks, Starr, Webb, Zapata (South Texas — trophy class)
Kerr, Gillespie, Llano, Mason, Edwards (Hill Country — highest density)

Trophy genetics counties:
Dimmit, Webb, Zavala, LaSalle, McMullen (South Texas free-range giants)
Kerr, Gillespie, Edwards, Real (Hill Country management deer)
Hudspeth, Culberson (Trans-Pecos mule deer)
