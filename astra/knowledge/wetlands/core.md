## THE THREE-PARAMETER APPROACH

A wetland requires ALL THREE parameters present during the growing season:
1. HYDROPHYTIC VEGETATION — plants adapted to saturated/flooded conditions
2. HYDRIC SOILS — soils formed under anaerobic conditions
3. WETLAND HYDROLOGY — inundation or saturation within 12 inches for >5% of growing season

Absence of any one parameter = not a wetland under Corps methodology.
All three present = wetland, regardless of NWI mapping status.
NWI maps wetlands — does not replace field delineation.
SSURGO hydric soils support wetland likelihood but do not confirm.

GROWING SEASON IN TEXAS:
North Texas (DFW): approximately March 15 — November 15
Central Texas: approximately March 1 — November 30
South Texas / Gulf Coast: approximately February 1 — December 15
Growing season determination affects hydrology criterion application.

## NWI COWARDIN CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

USFWS National Wetland Inventory uses Cowardin classification.
Each polygon coded: System + Subsystem + Class + Subclass + Modifiers.

SYSTEMS:
M — Marine: open ocean, coastal waters
E — Estuarine: tidal saltwater/brackish
R — Riverine: rivers, streams, channels
L — Lacustrine: lakes, ponds
P — Palustrine: inland wetlands (most common in Texas)

PALUSTRINE CLASSES (most relevant for Texas Phase I):
PEM — Palustrine Emergent: cattails, rushes, sedges, grasses
PFO — Palustrine Forested: bottomland hardwoods, cypress
PSS — Palustrine Scrub-Shrub: willows, buttonbush, shrubs
PUB — Palustrine Unconsolidated Bottom: open water wetlands
POW — Palustrine Open Water

WATER REGIME MODIFIERS (critical for hydrology assessment):
A — Temporarily Flooded: surface water present <12.5% of growing season
B — Saturated: substrate saturated >12.5% growing season, rarely flooded
C — Seasonally Flooded: surface water present 12.5-75% growing season
F — Semipermanently Flooded: surface water >75% growing season
H — Permanently Flooded: surface water present throughout year

SPECIAL MODIFIERS:
x — Excavated (dredged)
d — Diked/Impounded
f — Farmed (drained agricultural)
r — Artificially flooded

INTERPRETATION RULE:
Water regime modifier drives hydrology assessment.
A (temporarily flooded) = marginal hydrology, may not meet criterion
B (saturated) = hydrology likely met in wet years
C (seasonally flooded) = hydrology met, strong wetland
F/H (semi/permanently flooded) = definite wetland hydrology

## SECTION 404 JURISDICTION — POST-SACKETT (2023)

Sackett v. EPA (2023) fundamentally narrowed Clean Water Act jurisdiction.
This is the current controlling legal standard as of May 2026.

CURRENTLY JURISDICTIONAL:
1. Traditional navigable waters (TNW): large rivers, lakes used in commerce
2. Relatively permanent waters: perennial streams with continuous flow to TNW
3. Wetlands with continuous surface connection to TNW or relatively permanent water
   → Must have continuous surface water connection, not just hydrologic connection
   → Adjacency alone no longer sufficient post-Sackett

NO LONGER JURISDICTIONAL (post-Sackett):
1. Isolated wetlands with no surface connection to navigable water
2. Intermittent streams with only seasonal flow (in most circuits)
3. Wetlands adjacent to non-navigable tributaries without continuous surface connection
4. Ephemeral streams (flow only after rain events)

TEXAS JURISDICTION CONTEXT:
Texas is in the 5th Circuit — generally applies Sackett narrowly.
Many isolated playa lakes and prairie potholes in North/West Texas = NOT jurisdictional post-Sackett
Bottomland hardwoods along Trinity, Brazos, Colorado = likely jurisdictional (surface connection)
Isolated stock ponds = generally not jurisdictional
Roadside ditches with surface discharge to navigable water = jurisdiction fact-specific

PRACTICAL IMPLICATION FOR PHASE I:
Must note NWI wetlands present AND assess likely post-Sackett jurisdiction.
Statement should be: "Wetland indicators present. Jurisdictional determination requires
Corps of Engineers delineation and approved JD. Post-Sackett jurisdiction uncertain
for [isolated / intermittent] features."
Never say features are jurisdictional without Corps-approved JD.
Never say features are not jurisdictional — EP not authorized to make that determination.

APPROVED JURISDICTIONAL DETERMINATION (AJD):
Only the Corps of Engineers can issue an AJD.
AJD is site-specific, valid for 5 years.
Preliminary JD: conservative assumption of jurisdiction for permitting purposes.
Requestor may request AJD to challenge preliminary JD.

## SECTION 404 PERMIT TYPES

### Nationwide Permits (NWPs)
Pre-authorized for activities with minimal individual and cumulative effects.
Key NWPs for Texas development:

NWP 12: Utility line activities. Linear pipelines, cables, conduits.
  Limit: 0.1 acre of non-tidal wetland fill, no more than 300 linear feet of stream.

NWP 14: Linear transportation. Roads, bridges, access roads.
  Limit: 0.1 acre non-tidal wetland fill, 300 linear feet of stream.

NWP 18: Minor discharges. Small fills for minor activities.
  Limit: 0.1 acre of wetland fill, 25 cubic yards of material.

NWP 29: Residential developments.
  Limit: 0.5 acre of non-tidal wetland fill, 300 linear feet of stream.

NWP 39: Commercial and institutional developments.
  Limit: 0.5 acre of non-tidal wetland fill, 300 linear feet of stream.

NWP 51: Land-based renewable energy generation.
  Limit: 0.5 acre non-tidal wetland fill.

PRECONSTRUCTION NOTIFICATION (PCN) required when:
- Fill exceeds 0.1 acre of non-tidal wetland
- Fill within 100-year floodplain
- Species listed under ESA may be affected
- Historic properties may be affected
- Discharge exceeds 25 cubic yards in a special aquatic site

NWP TIMELINE: PCN review typically 45 days. Standard NWP authorization within 45 days if no PCN required.

### Individual Permit (IP)
Required when impact exceeds NWP thresholds or Corps determines NWP inappropriate.
Most complex, time-consuming permit pathway.
IP Timeline: typically 12-24 months from application to authorization.
Requires: permit application, delineation, alternatives analysis, mitigation plan, public notice.
Environmental impact analysis under NEPA required.
Section 7 ESA consultation if listed species present.
Section 106 NHPA consultation if historic properties present.
Cost: $50,000-$200,000+ in consulting fees, not including mitigation.

### Letter of Permission (LOP)
Simplified process for minor impacts.
Used when impacts are minor and uncontroversial.
Timeline: typically 30-60 days.

## MITIGATION SEQUENCING

Section 404(b)(1) requires mitigation in this order:
1. AVOID: design project to completely avoid wetland impacts
2. MINIMIZE: reduce impact as much as practicable
3. COMPENSATE: mitigate unavoidable impacts through compensatory mitigation

COMPENSATORY MITIGATION OPTIONS:
Mitigation banking: purchase credits from approved mitigation bank.
  Preferred by Corps — most reliable, pre-approved.
  Texas mitigation bank credits: typically $30,000-$80,000 per credit (wetland acre equivalent).
  Credit ratios: typically 1:1 to 3:1 depending on wetland type and quality.

In-lieu fee (ILF): payment to approved ILF program, Corps allocates funds.
  Second preference. Less control over where mitigation occurs.

Permittee-responsible mitigation (PRM): applicant creates, restores, or enhances wetland.
  Least preferred — applicant bears full risk of mitigation failure.
  Requires monitoring for 5+ years, performance standards, financial assurance.

MITIGATION COST ESTIMATES (Texas):
Mitigation bank credits: $30,000-$80,000/credit
ILF payments: $40,000-$60,000/credit
PRM (design + construction + monitoring): $50,000-$150,000/acre
Total mitigation cost for 0.5 acre NWP fill: $25,000-$75,000 (bank credits)
Total mitigation cost for 2 acre IP fill: $150,000-$500,000+

## TEXAS WETLAND ECOLOGY

### Bottomland Hardwood Forests (BLH)
Most ecologically significant wetland type in Texas.
Location: Trinity, Brazos, Colorado, Sabine, Neches river corridors.
Dominant trees: water oak, willow oak, Nuttall oak, green ash, cottonwood, hackberry, pecan.
NWI code: PFO1C (palustrine, forested, broad-leaved deciduous, seasonally flooded).
Jurisdictional status: very likely post-Sackett (surface connection to rivers).
Mitigation ratio: typically 2:1 to 3:1 — high ecological value.
Development constraint: significant — BLH removal triggers major mitigation.

### Playa Lakes (Panhandle / West Texas)
Isolated, ephemeral, seasonal wetlands on flat uplands.
Fill with rainwater, dry seasonally.
Jurisdictional status: UNCERTAIN post-Sackett — isolated, no surface connection.
Pre-Sackett many were jurisdictional under "significant nexus" test — post-Sackett likely not.
Important for migratory waterfowl — may trigger MBTA concerns even if not §404.
NWI code: PUBF or PEMF (seasonally to semi-permanently flooded).

### Coastal Marshes (Gulf Coast)
Saltwater and freshwater marshes along Gulf Coast.
Spartina alterniflora (smooth cordgrass) dominant in saline marsh.
Typha, Phragmites in fresh/brackish.
Jurisdictional: YES — direct surface connection to Gulf of Mexico.
Section 10 AND Section 404 permits required (navigable waters).
High ecological value — mitigation ratios 3:1 to 5:1.

### Prairie Potholes (North-Central Texas)
Seasonal wetlands in depressions on upland prairie.
Important waterfowl habitat — migratory stopover.
Jurisdictional status: uncertain post-Sackett — evaluate connectivity.
NWI: PEMC or PEMF.

### Cypress Swamps (East Texas)
Taxodium distichum (bald cypress) dominated.
Perennial hydrology — jurisdictional (connected to East Texas river systems).
High ecological value, slow to restore — high mitigation ratios.

### Riparian Corridors (statewide)
Wetlands and waters along stream banks.
Willows, cottonwood, sycamore dominant.
Jurisdictional if perennial stream with TNW connection — fact-specific for intermittent.

## DELINEATION METHODOLOGY

Controlling documents:
- Corps 1987 Wetland Delineation Manual (primary)
- Great Plains Regional Supplement (most of Texas)
- Atlantic and Gulf Coastal Plain Supplement (East Texas, coastal)

STANDARD METHOD (normal conditions):
All three parameters present in same location during growing season.
Vegetation: dominance test — >50% cover by OBL/FACW/FAC species.
Soils: hydric indicators present in profile (see soils domain).
Hydrology: indicators of wetland hydrology within 12 inches.

HYDROLOGY INDICATORS (positive if two or more present):
Primary: watermarks, drift lines, sediment deposits, oxidized rhizospheres.
Secondary: water-stained leaves, moss trim lines, dry-season water table within 12 inches.

ATYPICAL SITUATIONS:
Disturbed wetlands: hydrology altered by ditching, filling, or grading — requires judgment.
Problematic soils: hydric soils may not be present even with wetland hydrology (recently formed).
Drought conditions: hydrology criterion difficult to establish, representative data required.

PHASE I REMOTE ASSESSMENT PROTOCOL:
Step 1: NWI query within 500m of site.
Step 2: SSURGO hydric soils query.
Step 3: FEMA flood zone assessment.
Step 4: Historical aerial review for ponding or wet signatures.
Step 5: Conclude with likelihood statement and field delineation recommendation.

NEVER say: "No wetlands present" based on desktop assessment alone.
ALWAYS say: "No wetlands mapped within [radius] of site per USFWS NWI.
Field delineation is required to confirm absence of jurisdictional wetlands."

## PERMIT TIMELINE AND DEVELOPMENT IMPACT

PERMITTING TIMELINE SUMMARY:
No wetlands (confirmed by field delineation): no permit, no delay.
NWP with no PCN required: no delay, standard notification only.
NWP with PCN required: 45-day review, 6-8 week total delay.
NWP with PCN and agency coordination: 3-6 month delay.
Individual Permit (IP): 12-24 month delay minimum.
IP with ESA or NHPA consultation: 18-36 month delay possible.

DEVELOPMENT CONSTRAINT LEVELS:
NWI wetland on-site, isolated (post-Sackett probably not jurisdictional):
  → Low constraint. Field delineation recommended. State permit may still apply.
NWI wetland adjacent, connected to stream:
  → Moderate constraint. Delineation required. NWP likely if <0.5 acre.
BLH or riparian wetland on-site, connected:
  → High constraint. Delineation required. IP possible if >0.5 acre impact.
Coastal wetland on-site:
  → Very high constraint. Section 10 + 404 permits. 18-24 month timeline possible.

TEXAS STATE PROGRAM:
Texas does not have a state 404 program — USACE Fort Worth District has primary jurisdiction.
TCEQ Section 401 Water Quality Certification required for federal permits.
TCEQ 401 adds 30-60 days to IP process.
Texas General Land Office (GLO) has jurisdiction over coastal public lands.

## COMMON ERRORS IN WETLAND ASSESSMENT

1. Relying solely on NWI — NWI misses 20-30% of actual wetlands
2. Assuming all NWI wetlands are jurisdictional post-Sackett — incorrect
3. Saying no wetlands present based on desktop — requires field delineation
4. Missing isolated playa lakes that are not on NWI
5. Ignoring post-Sackett jurisdiction change — significant error
6. Not noting AJD requirement — EP cannot make jurisdictional determination
7. Missing MBTA implications for playa lakes and waterfowl habitat
8. Not distinguishing NWP vs IP permit pathway for development planning
9. Underestimating mitigation cost in deal impact assessment
10. Not noting 401 certification requirement for federal permits
11. Missing state GLO jurisdiction for coastal projects
12. Applying pre-Sackett case law to current jurisdiction analysis
13. Not checking for isolated wetlands outside NWI using aerial imagery
14. Confusing hydric soils with wetland presence — three parameters required
