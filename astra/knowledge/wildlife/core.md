## ENDANGERED SPECIES ACT — FRAMEWORK

### Section 7 — Federal Nexus Consultation
Applies when a federal agency is involved (federal permit, federal funding, federal land).
Federal agency must consult with USFWS if action may affect listed species.
Result: Biological Opinion (BO) — may include Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs).
Incidental Take Statement: Authorizes incidental take of listed species.

### Section 10 — Incidental Take Permit
For private actions with no federal nexus that may incidentally take listed species.
Requires Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP).
More burdensome than Section 7 — avoid if possible through project design.

### Key Definitions
Take: To harass, harm, pursue, hunt, shoot, wound, kill, trap, capture, or collect.
Harm: Significant habitat modification that kills or injures wildlife.
    → Habitat destruction CAN constitute take even without direct killing.
Harass: Actions that create likelihood of injury by disrupting normal behavior.

### Critical Habitat
Designated areas essential to conservation of listed species.
Two types:
- Primary constituent elements (PCEs): Physical or biological features essential to species
- Essential habitat: Areas where species currently occur OR areas needed for recovery

Adverse modification of critical habitat = separate ESA violation from take.
Project in or near critical habitat = Section 7 consultation almost certain.

## TEXAS ESA-LISTED SPECIES — KEY SPECIES FOR PHASE I

### Golden-Cheeked Warbler (Setophaga chrysoparia)
Status: Endangered (federally listed 1990)
Habitat: Mature Ashe juniper-oak woodland (Hill Country and Edwards Plateau)
  Requires Ashe juniper with shaggy bark for nesting material (strips bark for nest)
  Minimum habitat patch: Generally 10+ acres of contiguous woodland
  Elevation: Typically 1,200-2,500 feet in Hill Country
Range: Central Texas — Kerr, Kendall, Comal, Blanco, Gillespie, Travis, Hays,
  Bandera, Real, Edwards, and surrounding counties
Critical Habitat: Designated in 19 Central Texas counties
Nesting season: March 1 - July 31 — SURVEY SEASON
Avoidance window: If ground disturbance planned in occupied habitat during
  nesting season — USFWS consultation required

Phase I flag: If site is in or adjacent to mapped critical habitat in Hill Country,
flag as potential Section 7 trigger. Recommend pre-project survey if in suitable habitat.

### Black-Capped Vireo (Vireo atricapilla)
Status: Delisted 2018 (recovered) — monitor for re-listing
Former range: Oklahoma, Texas, Mexico
Texas: Primarily Edwards Plateau — Kerr, Kimble, Real, Val Verde counties
Note: Delisted but still monitor — habitat requirements overlap with GCW

### Houston Toad (Bufo houstonensis)
Status: Endangered (listed 1970)
Habitat: Sandy soils with loblolly pine and/or hardwood forest
Range: Bastrop, Burleson, Colorado, Austin, Lee, Robertson, Milam counties
Critical: Bastrop County — lost significant habitat in 2011 wildfires
Breeding: February-March in ephemeral ponds and flooded areas
Phase I flag: Any site in Bastrop County with sandy soils and forest cover —
  assess for Houston Toad habitat, coordinate with USFWS Austin office

### Whooping Crane (Grus americana)
Status: Endangered
Habitat: Winters on Texas Gulf Coast — Aransas National Wildlife Refuge
Migration corridor: Platte River to Texas coast along Central Flyway
Phase I flag: Coastal Texas sites near Aransas Bay and San Antonio Bay —
  any development affecting wintering habitat = USFWS coordination required
Conservation significance: Only ~500+ wild birds remaining

### Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle (Lepidochelys kempii)
Status: Endangered
Habitat: Gulf of Mexico, nests on Texas and Mexico beaches
Nesting beaches: South Padre Island, Padre Island National Seashore
Phase I flag: Coastal development projects affecting beach habitat

### Interior Least Tern (Sternula antillarum)
Status: Delisted 2021 (recovered)
Was listed: River sandbars along Trinity, Brazos, Red River systems
Note: Delisted but still ecologically significant — sandbars still protected by
  other provisions if in navigable waters

### Neotropical Migratory Birds
Not ESA-listed but protected under Migratory Bird Treaty Act (MBTA).
Any intentional killing of migratory birds = federal violation.
Construction timing near active nests = liability.
Key migration corridors: Central Flyway through Texas
High-risk periods: Spring migration (March-May), Fall migration (August-October)
Recommended: Pre-construction nest surveys during nesting season

### Texas Horned Lizard (Phrynosoma cornutum)
Status: Threatened under Texas law (not federally listed)
State protection: TPWD — cannot take, possess, or sell
Habitat: Open areas with loose sandy soils, harvester ant colonies
Range: Statewide but declining — West Texas and plains most common

## TPWD STATE PROTECTION — TEXAS SPECIES

Texas Parks and Wildlife Department protects species under state law.
State threatened and endangered list differs from federal ESA list.

Key TPWD-listed species:
- Texas horned lizard (Threatened)
- Timber rattlesnake (Threatened — populations in East Texas)
- Alligator snapping turtle (Threatened)
- Texas salamander species (multiple listed)
- Texas blind salamander (Endangered — Edwards Aquifer dependent)
- Various freshwater mussel species (Endangered — North Texas rivers)

Texas Freshwater Mussels — Trinity River System:
Multiple listed mussel species in Trinity River and tributaries.
Any project affecting stream substrate in Trinity system =
TPWD coordination recommended.
Species: Texas pimpleback, Texas fawnsfoot, smooth pimpleback.

## CRITICAL HABITAT — PHASE I ASSESSMENT

For Phase I desktop assessment:

Step 1: Check USFWS IPaC (Information, Planning, and Conservation tool)
  ipac.ecosphere.fws.gov — enter project coordinates, get species list
  This is the official USFWS tool for Section 7 pre-consultation screening

Step 2: Critical habitat mapping
  USFWS critical habitat portal — map by species and location
  GCW critical habitat is mapped by 7.5-minute quad in Hill Country

Step 3: Texas Wildlife Action Plan
  TPWD maintains species of greatest conservation need (SGCN) maps
  Even non-listed species SGCN areas may require coordination

Step 4: Central Flyway context
  Sites along major waterfowl migration corridors in North Texas —
  especially Trinity River corridor, Lewisville Lake, Lake Ray Hubbard,
  Richland-Chambers Reservoir = waterfowl habitat of regional significance

## WATERFOWL AND CONSERVATION — BLUE DUCK CONTEXT

Central Flyway waterfowl significance:
Texas is the most important wintering state for Central Flyway waterfowl.
Major species: Mallard, Northern Pintail, Green-winged Teal, Gadwall,
  American Wigeon, Northern Shoveler, Ring-necked Duck, Lesser Scaup.

Northern Pintail: Below population goal since 1970s — highly sensitive.
  Any habitat conservation opportunity in pintail range = high priority.

NAWCA (North American Wetlands Conservation Act) grant priorities:
  Focus on habitat protection and restoration in Central Flyway.
  Eligible projects: Wetland protection, restoration, enhancement.
  Match requirement: Non-federal match required (1:1 standard grant).

Conservation easement value for waterfowl:
  Seasonal wetlands (playas, moist soil units) = highest waterfowl value.
  Permanent water = year-round habitat but lower diversity value.
  Moist soil management: Draw-down and reflooding mimics natural cycle.

## INVASIVE SPECIES — TEXAS CONTEXT

Invasive species affecting Phase I and conservation assessments:

Giant Reed (Arundo donax): Invasive along Texas rivers including Trinity.
  Displaces native riparian vegetation, reduces habitat value.
  Can indicate disturbed riparian areas.

Chinese Tallow (Triadica sebifera): Invasive tree in East Texas and coast.
  Rapidly colonizes disturbed wetland edges and floodplains.
  Indicator of wetland disturbance when dominant.

Saltcedar (Tamarix spp.): Invasive in West and Central Texas along streams.
  Increases salinity, consumes large amounts of water, reduces habitat.

Nutria (Myocastor coypus): South and East Texas wetlands.
  Herbivory can destroy emergent marsh vegetation rapidly.

Giant Salvinia (Salvinia molesta): Invasive aquatic plant.
  East Texas lakes and streams — can completely cover water surface.
  TPWD actively manages.

## COMMON ERRORS IN WILDLIFE ASSESSMENT

1. Missing MBTA nesting season: Clearing vegetation during active nesting = violation.
   Always recommend pre-construction nest surveys March-August.

2. Assuming no listed species without IPaC check: Always run IPaC for any site
   in Hill Country, Bastrop County, coastal areas, or Trinity River corridor.

3. Confusing state and federal listing: Texas has state-listed species not on
   federal ESA. TPWD coordination needed for state-listed species.

4. Missing freshwater mussel context: Trinity River mussel species rarely
   mentioned in Phase I but can be significant for stream work.

5. Ignoring NAWCA and conservation context: Blue Duck Foundation's conservation
   mission aligns with NAWCA priorities — always assess waterfowl habitat value
   for any wetland or floodplain site.

6. Missing migratory bird timing: Seasonal vulnerability windows critical.
   Construction timing can avoid most MBTA liability.
